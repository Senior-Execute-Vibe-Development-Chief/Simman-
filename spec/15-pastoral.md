# 15 — Herds and the emergent steppe  `[DESIGN DETAIL — ratified DECISIONS 12]`

There is no nomad type. There are herds, grass, and the standing rule that
communities drift toward their food.

## 15.1 The physics (12a)

- **Herds** are per-community stocks (energy converters: grass/fodder/
  grain → milk, meat, wool, traction, mounts), in the conserved energy
  books (04.2).
- **Grass** is a seasonal resource: per-tile pasture productivity by month
  (from the monthly climate), **locally depleted by grazing and regrowing
  seasonally** — a herd larger than local regrowth must move. That single
  fact is why pastoral mobility exists.
- **Three feeding strategies**, priced by the books: graze (walk the herd
  to grass), fodder (hay — cut, stored grass: labor + a storage
  technique; enables overwintering in place), grain (expensive — competes
  with human mouths).
- **Communities drift toward their food source** (the general rule, not a
  pastoral special case). Where farming anchors the food supply, the
  community stays and at most its herders ride circuits (transhumance).
  Where cropping fails and grass is the economy, the whole community
  tracks the herd. Portable dwellings are rendering (12), not state.
- The whole spectrum — mixed farming, alpine transhumance, agro-pastoral
  borderlands, full steppe nomadism — is one continuum on the feeding
  ratio. The **agro-pastoral frontier emerges at the rainfall line where
  cropping beats herding in calories per labor** — reality check: in a
  China-shaped region this line must track where the Great Wall stood.

## 15.2 Steppe society, derived

- Herd wealth is mobile: raidable, not taxable-in-place → steppe authority
  is personal following (pure chain, zero territorial administration —
  Seshat's near-zero admin depth at vast reach).
- Herding is daily riding → the war-domain practice volume (11) of an
  entire adult population; with low density and no siegecraft practice:
  raid superiority, conquest difficulty. The trade-raid toggle (grain and
  cloth for horses, or extortion) follows 13's assessment logic — tribute
  edges pointing from empire to horde are expected outcomes.

## 15.3 Confederation and conquest (12b, 12c)

- **Mirror-empire cascade**: a successful raiding center's following grows
  with distributed loot; the cascade hazard of clans joining scales with
  the **adjacent settled neighbor's extortable wealth**. Poor neighbors →
  feuding mist; a golden empire next door → a horde, sometimes in one
  generation. Person-bound edges (05.4) make it shatter at succession.
- **The dismount dilemma**: when conquest succeeds (weak settled side,
  confederated steppe), the conqueror's legitimacy books split — steppe
  followers eroded by every settled institution adopted (11's
  administration domain, whose carriers are the conquered), settled
  subjects built by the same. Rule from horseback and die with the khan,
  or dismount, endure, soften, and be replaced off the grass — the
  Khaldun cycle on a ~3–4 generation beat, emergent.
- The steppe as corridor: grass is cheap for riders — techniques, goods,
  and plagues move fast along it (travel field + 11 transmission, free).
