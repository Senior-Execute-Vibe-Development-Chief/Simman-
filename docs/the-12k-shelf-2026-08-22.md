# The 12k shelf: why two-thirds of the world's cities are identical

Owner, 2026-08-22: *"EVERY SINGLE CITY with a population of EXACTLY 12k is
starving. i am almost 100% sure this is because they CANNOT become smaller
than 12k."* Then: *"towns should not exist, anything smaller than a city
should not be anything."*

Both observations were right, and the measurement at the grid that ships
is worse than either suggested.

## Measured (probe_corepile, tw=960, full live stack, step 35,000)

```
cities=1104   starving=489 (44%)   SUB-CITY(<10k core)=278
urbanCore:  p10=2.1k   p50=12.0k   p90=12.0k   max=105k
modal buckets:  12k:704   2k:53   1k:40   0k:31   5k:24   8k:23
core/ownBound:  p50=1.00  p90=1.00   AT-BOUND(0.9-1.1)=717 (65%)
```

**The median city, the 90th-percentile city, and 704 of 1,104 cities are
all exactly 12,000 people.** `core/ownBound = 1.00` at both the 50th and
90th percentile: two-thirds of the world's cities are resting precisely on
`_coreHoldCapF = coreBarF × 1.2` — the birth floor, to the digit. Only the
extreme tail (max 105k) ever escapes it.

This is a KIND divergence from the reference grid, where the same code
gives p50 = 105k and only 1% at the bound (docs/runs corepile480). The
Third Cardinal Rule again: the shelf is invisible at tw=240/480 and
dominant at tw=960.

## Root cause: urban growth is 100% import-driven

`popField.js deriveOnePop`:

```js
let uTarget = 0;
if (agglom && kBeyond > 0 && sumKb > 0) { ... }   // kBeyond = the IMPORT ceiling
let kCap = agglom ? uTarget : kBeyond;
```

The comment states the intent plainly: *"Non-importers (kBeyond=0) have no
target — they stay rural."* So a city that feeds its core from its OWN
hinterland has **zero** urban capacity. The only thing holding it up is the
CORE_HOLD birth floor — and that floor is exactly 12k. Hence the shelf: at
tw=960 roughly two-thirds of cities are non-importers, so they are pinned
at their birth size forever, and 44% of them are starving there.

**That is historically backwards.** A city grows on its own catchment's
surplus as the normal case; imports are what let it EXCEED its hinterland,
not what let it exist above the mint bar. Rome needed Egyptian grain only
at a million people. A 50,000-person city on the North China Plain or in
the Nile valley fed itself from the land it stood on. Under the current
law, no such city can exist: without a trade surplus it is frozen at 12k.

This one mechanism produces four of the owner's standing complaints:
"slop cities with exactly 12k citizens"; "vast majority say they are
starving but never shrink"; the flat, non-Zipf size distribution; and the
register's inability to grow real metropolises outside trade hubs.

The law works exactly as designed — 159 husks per window now fade back
into countryside instead of persisting forever, and the sub-city
population falls. It does NOT touch the shelf, and cannot: 12,000 urban
people is a legitimate city by this sim's own bar and by history's. The
owner was told this before the arms landed; it is confirmed.

## The named next wave: a city grows on its own hinterland

The fix is not to raise the floor or lower the bar — both would be fitting
the outcome. It is the missing MECHANISM: urban capacity must have a
LOCAL-SURPLUS term, so a city's core can grow on the food its own
catchment produces above what that catchment eats, with the import economy
extending it beyond the local limit exactly as it does today. That
restores the historical ordering (self-fed cities are normal; trade-fed
giants are exceptional), and it should collapse the shelf into a real
distribution without touching a single bar.

Measurement to run first: what share of the 12k cities have a genuine
local surplus their core is not allowed to eat? If it is large, the shelf
is pure accounting; if it is small, the cities really are on poor land and
the honest outcome is that they DIE rather than grow — which the new core
bar now permits.
