### 1. Base-layer cache canvas is overwritten by non-cached views without invalidating its key — visiting an identity lens corrupts every cached lens
`src/WorldSim.jsx:1670` · **CRITICAL** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `ui-render`

Both branches of the base blit write into the SAME offscreen canvas, but only the cached branch updates the key: `if(_staticBase){ ... _bl.getContext('2d').putImageData(img,0,0);ctx.drawImage(_bl,0,0);baseLayerKey.current=_baseKey; } else { // Non-cached views: route the image data through a temp canvas ... _bl.getContext('2d').putImageData(img,0,0);ctx.drawImage(_bl,0,0); }` (lines 1660-1673). ### 2. Settlement/colonisation spread rate is ramped on raw step count (COVERAGE_RAMP) — a textbook time gate
`src/sim/peopleSim/crystallize.js:278` · **CRITICAL** · Cardinal rule 1 (time-gating) · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `territory-identity`

`const devFactor = Math.min(1, COVERAGE_FLOOR + (1 - COVERAGE_FLOOR) * (world.step * (world._dt || 1)) / COVERAGE_RAMP);` with `COVERAGE_FLOOR = 0.22` and `COVERAGE_RAMP = 17000` (comment: "steps over which the spread rate ramps to full (~renaissance)"). devFactor multiplies BOTH the crystallisation probability (line 437, `const p = quality * ... * devFactor ...`) and the colony-send chance (line 795, `if (rng() >= COLONY_CHANCE * colonySat * devFactor) continue;`). This is precisely the CLAUDE.md "WRONG: if (world.step > 12000) openFrontier()" pattern — how fast the wilderness recedes is a piece of history, and it is being driven by the tick counter, not by what the world has become. Failure scenario (the two-clock trap, verbatim from the project's own scar tissue): a seed whose tech stalls in the neolithic still gets its settlement-spread rate ramped to 100% by step 17000, so a stone-age world fills wall-to-wall at renaissance pace; conversely a fast-developing world is throttled to 22% spread while its states are already classical empires starving for the villages that reach-adoption feeds on. Notably index.js's own comment (line ~99) brags that the era gates for "frontier close, hinterland claim, identity salience" were de-pinned onto the emergent `_civYear` — this ramp was missed.

> **Verifier correction:** Exactly as claimed, with one nuance: the ramp input is `world.step * world._dt` (elapsed history-time, granularity-normalized per index.js:166), not raw ticks — but that is still a pure clock, not emergent state, so the violation stands. devFactor (crystallize.js:278) multiplies both the crystallisation probability (line 437) and, via maybeSendSettlers(world, _alive, devFactor) at line 290 → line 795, the colony-send chance. index.js:126-134 and 184-194 confirm sibling gates (frontier close, hinterland claim, identity salience) were already de-pinned onto world._civYear/_leadOrg, which are computed every pass and available for the proposed fix.

**Fix:** Drive the coverage tempo from emergent development, not the step count: e.g. scale devFactor from the DONOR settlement's own development (agriculture/organization of the nearest settlement that would seed the site), or from `world._leadOrg`/`_civYear` (already computed each tick in index.js from the leading capital's organisation) — `devFactor = f(civYearFromOrg-style mapping of local development)`. Then a stalled world stays a sparse frontier forever and a precocious one fills early, per the cardinal rule.


## HIGH (32)

### 3. Random and Pangaea presets have a hard discontinuity wall at the E-W wrap seam
`src/sim/worldgen.js:599` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `worldgen-tectonics`

The sim treats X as toroidal everywhere (dirDist 'X wraps (toroidal)', all BFS passes wrap x), but the default 'random' preset's rawElev loop (lines 599-631) and the pangaea loop (540-561) are built from Perlin fbm sampled at nx=x/W, which is NOT periodic: `e+=fbm(wnx*7+3.7,wny*7+3.7,4,2,.5)*.10` etc. differ at nx=0 vs nx=1, and the domain-warp offsets (w1x/w1y, ±0.08) shift even the wrap-corrected stamp field differently on each side of the seam. tectonicGen.js knows about this and cross-fades the seam twice (lines 333-349 and 1121-1134); worldgen.js never does. Probe at 480x240, seed 8817: default preset mean |elev[x=0]-elev[x=W-1]| = 0.0271 vs 0.0021 for an interior column pair (13x), with 41 of 240 rows flipping land<->ocean exactly at the seam (vs 2 rows interior); pangaea: 0.0587 vs 0.0023 (25x). Tectonic preset: 0.0004 (blended, fine). Moisture and temperature use the same non-periodic fbm terms (lines 682, 695-696), so a climate wall sits on the same column. Result: any random/pangaea world has a fake north-south cliff/coast/climate front at the wrap column that rivers, settlement, and armies will interact with.

> **Verifier correction:** Claim holds as stated; minor corrections: interior baseline column diffs measure 0.0005 (random) and 0.0016 (pangaea) with 3 interior land/ocean flips (not 0.0021/0.0023 and 2), making the seam ratios ~54x and ~37x — worse than claimed. Also note the positive/negative stamps DO wrap dx (lines 608/615), so the wall comes from the additive non-periodic fbm terms and the non-periodic domain-warp (w1x/w1y, line 601), exactly as the finding says.

**Fix:** Apply the same antimeridian cross-fade tectonicGen uses (blend a ~3%-width strip of rawElev, moisture and temperature toward the two-sided average), or make the noise periodic in x (sample noise on a cylinder: use angle-based coordinates cos/sin(2*pi*nx)).

### 4. Zonal-mean winds are easterly at ALL latitudes — the mid-latitude westerlies never form
`src/sim/windSolver.js:203` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `climate`

Pressure is built as `const meridionalP = -slt * _pressureScale;` (line 203) plus belt structure `cellP * _pressureScale` where the belts carry `_hadleyStr = 0.08`. Sea-level temperature falls ~0.4 units between 30° and 60° latitude, so meridionalP RISES ~0.33 poleward across that band, while the entire subtropical-high-to-subpolar-low dip (0.8·0.08 + 0.5·0.08 ≈ 0.10, before the same _pressureScale) is ~3× smaller. Surface pressure therefore increases monotonically toward both poles, and geostrophic balance yields easterlies everywhere. Probe (all-ocean 480×240 world, defaults): zonal-mean u = -0.076 at 45°N, -0.114 at 60°N, -0.168 at 45°S — easterly at every latitude tested; real Earth has strong westerlies at 35-60°. Consequence: every solver-wind world (tectonic mode and earth_sim without the real-wind checkbox) advects mid-latitude moisture and temperature from the EAST — west coasts (the W-Europe analogue) get continental upwind air. The codebase visibly compensates downstream with hand-painted westerly mechanisms in worldgen.js (the eastward warm-air running sweep at line 231, westFetch continentality, the wind-independent distToOcean storm-track rain in moistureSolver) — a textbook Second-Cardinal-Rule cascade: the mechanism is broken, so its outcomes were painted on by hand.

> **Verifier correction:** Minor numeric corrections only: sea-level temperature falls ~0.50 (not ~0.4) between 30° and 60° (latTemp 0.834→0.334), making the meridional pressure rise ~0.41 — about 5× (not ~3×) the belt dip of ~0.085; the claim is understated. Probe values match exactly (-0.076 at 45N, -0.114 at 60N, -0.168 at 45S).

**Fix:** Fix the mechanism, not the outputs: drastically flatten meridionalP (real annual SLP is nearly flat equator-to-pole outside the belts; the thermal-direct component of SLP is weak) or scale it down ~5-10× while raising _hadleyStr so the subtropical-high/subpolar-low dip dominates the 30-60° band; verify with the all-ocean probe that zonal-mean u > 0 in 35-60° and < 0 in the trades, then retire the hand-coded westerly patches in worldgen as they become redundant.

### 5. Seasonal monsoon heating is applied to the WRONG hemisphere (July heats Australia, cools Asia)
`src/sim/windSolver.js:110` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `climate`

latFrac = (wy/wH - 0.5)*2 is -1 at the TOP row, which is 90°N (realWindData.js:71 and worldgen's shCool both fix top = north). So `const hemi = latFrac >= 0 ? 1 : -1;` gives hemi=+1 in the SOUTHERN hemisphere, and `T += _season * hemi * lf * 0.30 * latW;` makes season=+1 (documented at lines 38-42 and 104-108 as 'boreal summer (July)... July heats Asia and cools Australia') heat SOUTHERN land and cool northern land — the exact inverse of the comment. Probe confirms: with identical land blocks at 15-45°N and 15-45°S, season=+1 yields net wind OUTFLOW from the northern block (-11.7) and INFLOW into the southern block (+7.3); season=-1 the reverse. worldgen.js:266/277 pairs solveWind({season:1}) with solveMoisture({itczLat:+13}) as 'boreal summer', so the boreal-summer moisture solve runs January-like offshore continental winds over Asia while its ITCZ rain belt sits at 13°N — the monsoon inflow and the monsoon rain belt are in opposite half-years. The scar is visible: worldgen.js:493-505 documents that 'the solver's summer wind there blows ZONALLY (and even offshore against the Himalaya) ... so Bengal/the Ganges come out arid' and patches it with a hard-coded lat/lon wet floor over the Gangetic plain — a named-region fit papering over this sign error.

> **Verifier correction:** All specifics check out as stated; one additional supporting note: windSolver.js:165-168 places the annual-mean ITCZ pressure trough at latDegSigned=+8, i.e. 8°S under the top=north convention, suggesting the same latFrac-sign confusion pervades the file (though latW≈0 near the equator makes that instance minor).

**Fix:** Flip the sign: `const hemi = latFrac >= 0 ? -1 : 1;` (or equivalently negate the added term). Then re-validate the earth_sim monsoon and try removing the Bengal-foreland geographic patch in worldgen.js — with correct onshore summer flow it should become largely unnecessary.

### 6. Real-NCEP wind path: meridional component sign-inverted (v is northward-positive, internal windY is southward-positive)
`src/realWindData.js:158` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `climate`

fillRealWind writes `windY[i] = v * scale;` with no axis flip, and tools/convert_wind_data.py does no flip either — NCEP vwnd is standard meteorological v, positive = toward the NORTH. The internal convention is screen-y (positive = SOUTH): windSolver's Hadley overturning (lines 288-296) adds positive windY in the north to mean 'equatorward near surface', and moistureSolver's backward trace `srcY = my - dirY * cellReach` (line 221) fetches from larger y (further south) when windY < 0. So every real-wind meridional component is mirrored north-south: NE trades become SE-ward flow whose moisture trace samples the wrong side, summer onshore southerlies read as offshore northerlies, Hadley surface convergence becomes divergence. When the user enables the Earth-Sim real-wind checkbox (worldgen.js:258-263 feeds these arrays straight into both solveMoisture calls and the temperature advection), the entire meridional half of the 'most physically grounded' path is backwards while u stays correct — a subtle, systematic parity break between the simulated-wind and real-wind paths.

> **Verifier correction:** Claim holds as stated; only the illustrative wording "NE trades become SE-ward flow" is imprecise — precisely, the NH trades' equatorward (southward, NCEP v<0) component maps to internal windY<0 = poleward flow, so the moisture trace samples from the south instead of the north. The fix (windY[i] = -v * scale in fillRealWind) is correct.

**Fix:** In fillRealWind (or once in sampleRealWind's return), negate v: `windY[i] = -v * scale;`. Add a parity probe comparing solver-wind vs real-wind meridional sign in the trade belt (both should be equatorward at the surface).

### 7. Lake tiles reach the sim as fertile, passable, resource-bearing land (never as water)
`src/sim/pipeline.js:403` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `hydro-pipeline`

Lakes are computed in riverGen and marked in pipeline at line 403: `if(rivers.lake[ti]>=0){tMoist[ti]=0.8;tFert[ti]=0;tDiff[ti]=1.0;}`. This zeroes tFert and makes tDiff impassable — but neither tFert nor tDiff is passed to the people-sim. The sim receives tCrop, tFlood, deposits, w.elevation, w.moisture. Two problems: (1) tCrop (Pass 3, lines 509-551) runs AFTER the lake override, so it reads the lake's tMoist=0.8 and elevation>0 and computes a normal crop value; a lake tile that was earlier flagged tFlood (arid-river floodplain, line 379, which runs before the lake override) even gets the 0.92 floodplain override at line 532. I verified on earth_sim W=480 seed=4242: of 464 lake tiles, 401 have tCrop>0 (avg 0.35, MAX 0.92). (2) generateResources (line 554) only skips `e<=0`; lake tiles have e>0 and tMoist=0.8, so getBiome classifies them as forest/rainforest and seeds deposits — I measured 456 of 464 lake tiles carrying a deposit, 436 with timber. The lake array itself is NEVER passed to peopleSim (worker init sends `rivers:{riverMag}` only, WorldSim.jsx:967; initPeopleSim opts carry no lake mask; grep shows peopleSim reads only w.rivers.riverMag). Net: settlements can crystallize on, farm, and mine open-water lake surfaces because the only lake signal (tFert=0, tDiff=1) lives in arrays the sim never sees.

> **Verifier correction:** Empirical re-run (earth_sim W=480 seed=4242) gives 402 lake tiles with tCrop>0 (reviewer said 401; avg 0.351, max 0.920 matches), 456/464 with deposits, 436 timber, all 464 with tElev>0, and 125 flagged tFlood (so the 0.92 floodplain override at pipeline.js:532 does fire on lakes).

**Fix:** Zero tCrop and every deposit on lake tiles as part of the lake override (e.g. after Pass 3, set tCrop[ti]=0 and clear deposits for lake tiles), OR pass a lake/water mask into initPeopleSim and have the sim treat lake tiles as impassable water like ocean. Also move the lake override before tFlood assignment so a lake never inherits the floodplain 0.92.

### 8. Unclamped logistic step annihilates a large settlement in one tick (and stores negative population) when its food K collapses
`src/sim/peopleSim/settlement.js:2253` · **HIGH** · Bug · verdict: **CONFIRMED** · found by `settlement`

updatePopulation integrates growth with a raw Euler step: `s.people = s.people + T.SETT_GROWTH * _dt * s.people * (1 - s.people / K)`. Under the default model (DISSOLVE_FARMS=1) K = `Math.max(K_MIN_VIABLE, foodK)` with K_MIN_VIABLE=8 and foodK = `_foodSupply / perCapita` recomputed every tick. If a city's supply collapses (war front captures its catchment so `_terrWorkTiles`/`_terrFertSum` go ~0 on the next territory pass, or its food-hierarchy subtree is severed, so `_foodNet`≈0 and fish 0), K crashes to 8 immediately WHILE the granary is still full. The famine path (`if (s.food <= 0.01)` → 1.5%/tick die-off) is bypassed because food > 0.01, so the logistic branch runs with people/K in the hundreds. Concrete: people=5000, K=8, SETT_GROWTH=0.0018, _dt=1 → delta = 0.0018·5000·(1−625) = −5616 → s.people = −616 → `< 1.5` → mode="dead", event "settlement.abandoned" logged, and a NEGATIVE people value sits on the object until pruneDead (up to 32 ticks). Even sub-lethal ratios are wrong: people=2000, K=8 → −896 people/tick, i.e. a metropolis evaporates in 2-3 ticks with a full granary, instead of the intended granary-buffer-then-slow-famine arc. The whole designed starvation pipeline (storage cap, `s.food` drain, 0.985 die-off, wither timer) is dead code for any large-K-collapse event, which is exactly the dramatic case (sieges, catchment loss) it exists for.

> **Verifier correction:** One sub-scenario is overstated: severing the food-hierarchy subtree alone does not zero a town's supply under DISSOLVE_FARMS, since every town farms its own catchment (farmsLand, settlement.js:885) and a severed node keeps its own _storableSupply as a hierarchy root (foodHierarchy.js:130-133, 184). The real triggers are catchment capture/disconnection via the territory pass (territory.js tallyTerritory) or a near-total harvest shock (_harvestMul), which suffice for the claimed failure.

**Fix:** Clamp the decline so the relative per-tick change is bounded, e.g. `const r = T.SETT_GROWTH * _dt * (1 - s.people / K); s.people *= Math.exp(Math.max(-0.03 * _dt, Math.min(r, 0.05)))` or explicitly cap over-capacity decline at the famine rate: `if (s.people > K) s.people = Math.max(K, s.people * Math.pow(0.985, _dt)); else logistic`. Also assert/clamp `s.people >= 0` after the update.
