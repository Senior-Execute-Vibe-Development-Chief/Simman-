# Part 2 — How the app actually works (subsystem maps)


## worldgen-tectonics
_Files: src/sim/worldgen.js, src/sim/worldgenUtils.js, src/sim/tectonicGen.js_

generateWorld(W, H, seed, preset, oceanLevel, ..., _tecParams, realWindFns) in src/sim/worldgen.js is the single pure entry point for worldgen (run in worldGenWorker off-thread, and headlessly by ~60 tools/ probes). It seeds two deterministic sources — initNoise(seed) fills a module-level 512-entry Perlin permutation table in worldgenUtils.js that ALL noise functions (noise2D/fbm/warp/ridged/worley) read, and mkRng(seed) is a Park-Miller LCG for discrete choices — then branches by preset into one of five elevation/climate paths, all writing three Float32Array fields (elevation, signed: >0 land, <=0 ocean; moisture; temperature, both 0-1) plus derived coastal/swamp masks, returning {elevation, moisture, temperature, coastal, swamp, pixPlate, windX, windY, _seed}. Determinism is solid: I verified two same-seed runs produce bit-identical arrays. "earth" decodes a base64 1920x960 real DEM (earthData.js; ocean=byte 0 via border flood-fill so the Med/Black/Caspian are ocean), bilinearly resamples it, and paints climate from closed-form latitude curves plus a coarse (4px-tile) coast-distance BFS for continentality. "earth_sim" — the flagship — uses the same DEM but runs the full physical stack: carveStraits opens Gibraltar; directional distance sweeps (dirDist, a clever O(W·H) two-lap toroidal running sweep) build east-coast/open-ocean/west-fetch fields; a four-limb ocean-gyre SST-anomaly model (warm/cold western and eastern boundary currents keyed on coast direction, basin openness and latitude Gaussians) is spilled onto land isotropically plus an eastward westerly-advection sweep; solveWind+solveMoisture run TWICE (boreal summer/winter, ITCZ shifted ±13°) and the wetter half-year is kept, which is what produces the monsoon; a 60-iteration semi-Lagrangian temperature advection on a half-res grid adds current-driven flavor (8% blend); and a final per-pixel pass composes ~15 named drying/wetting terms (subtropical belt with monsoon/east-coast spares, savanna seasonality from the wet/dry contrast, plus explicit geographic corrections for Arabia, Patagonia, the Ganges foreland, interior Asia, the Andes LLJ).

"tectonic" (tectonicGen.js) is the emergent-world generator. It builds 11-15 plates as a warped weighted-Voronoi power diagram (log-normal-ish weights give Pacific-vs-Juan-de-Fuca size disparity; fbm+ridged+worley warping gives jagged boundaries; a cos-lat correction keeps plates round on the sphere), assigns random velocities, and grows continents as branching stamp-walks around each continental plate's nucleus — stamps mostly vanish off their own plate (plateFactor 0.02) so land shapes follow plate geometry. Sea level is a percentile cut (p('seaLevel',0.67)) of the stamp field; crustType (continental/oceanic) is DERIVED from the resulting land mask. Plate-boundary interactions are computed on a 4px coarse grid: relative velocity projected on the boundary normal classifies each boundary (cont-cont collision → 0.20 uplift propagated 60 cells by an in-plate BFS with a flat-core-plus-Gaussian falloff for Tibet-style plateaus; ocean-cont → 0.15/25 cells; rifts → lowering/10 cells), a sigma-14 blur makes the broad plateau field, and STEP 8 composes the final pixel elevation from ~12 layers (shield/basin/endorheic/escarpment noise in craton zones, ridged veining in tectonic zones, hypsometric gamma-1.38 redistribution, layered bathymetry). A droplet hydraulic-erosion pass (delta-clamped to -30% so it textures rather than flattens) runs on a 4x grid, then the whole map is cyclically rotated so the wrap seam lands on the emptiest ocean band (elevation, pixPlate, isLandArr AND cdist rotate together — a past bug, now fixed), the seam is cross-faded, and solveWind/solveMoisture/temperature-advection run on the rotated field. "pangaea" and the default "random" preset are simpler pure-noise/stamp compositions with closed-form climate. Everything here is time-free (worldgen runs once), so Cardinal Rule 1 is trivially satisfied; Rule 2 is satisfied by the tectonic path but knowingly bent by earth_sim's geographic patches (see findings).

Overall verdict: the code is in much better shape than its density suggests — determinism holds, the toroidal E-W wrap is handled correctly in nearly every kernel (BFS, dirDist, advection, blur, erosion all wrap x and clamp y at the poles), pole rows are consistently excluded from advection, and the ocean/land identity is carefully re-clamped after every noise layer so no pass can flip a tile's class. The significant problems found: the default/pangaea presets lack the antimeridian seam handling the tectonic path has (confirmed land/ocean flips at the wrap column); the plate convergence sign is inverted (mountains at separating boundaries — ensemble-invisible today but physically backwards); the plain "earth" preset never carves Gibraltar so the Mediterranean is naval-sealed; the seasonal monsoon machinery exists only for earth_sim, leaving emergent worlds monsoon-less; and initNoise corrupts its table on negative seeds.


## climate
_Files: src/sim/windSolver.js, src/sim/moistureSolver.js, src/sim/peopleSim/climate.js, src/realWindData.js_

Wind is solved once at worldgen time on a 4x-coarse grid (windSolver.js). STEP 1 builds a 0-centered surface temperature field from a latitude curve, a land-fraction thermal-contrast term, an optional seasonal land-sea swing (params.season = ±1, used only by earth_sim's two-season solve), lapse-rate cooling, and fbm noise. STEP 2 converts it to pressure: a dominant meridional term (-seaLevelTemp × pressureScale), Hadley/Ferrel/polar belt Gaussians × hadleyStrength (10x weaker), an ocean subtropical-high boost, a thermal anomaly vs the zonal mean (the monsoon engine: warm land → low), and two scales of synoptic noise. STEP 5 initializes with an analytic Ekman balance, adds prescribed Hadley/Ferrel meridional overturning, then runs 500 explicit-Euler iterations of PGF + Coriolis (f = ±coriolisStrength·sin(lat), correct sign for screen-down y) + linear drag + viscous diffusion, with a soft divergence projection every 10th iteration that also zeroes wind over terrain above wElev 0.3 (crude plateau blocking). Post-passes add gap funneling (off by default), curl-noise eddies (ocean only by default — landEddyStrength is 0), gust and curl boosts, and 80 passes of terrain deflection that removes the into-slope wind component and redirects it along-slope. The result is bilinearly upscaled (clamped, not wrapped — see findings) to full resolution. X wraps toroidally everywhere in the interior math; Y is clamped with zero-wind rows at both poles. My all-ocean probe shows the solver is numerically stable and converged by ~200-500 iterations, produces correct easterly trades with equatorward surface flow, but NO mid-latitude westerlies at any latitude — the meridional pressure term swamps the belt structure (finding 1).

Moisture (moistureSolver.js) runs on a 2x-coarse grid fed by that wind, elevation, and a temperature array that in practice is always still zeros, so an internal latitude→temperature estimate is what actually gates it (finding 9). Ocean cells hold a temperature- and wind-speed-driven evaporation value, reset every step (fixed boundary). Land runs 140 steps of depletive semi-Lagrangian advection: each cell backward-traces a fixed reach (~1.65°/step, resolution-normalized) along the normalized wind direction, bilinearly samples the previous field (X-wrapped, with the seam-safe fdx fix), halves it if the trace corner lands on ocean, then takes the max with a lossy isotropic neighbor spread (survival resolution-normalized to a fixed per-degree leak). The parcel is clamped to a Clausius-Clapeyron-style temperature capacity, then rains via orographic up-slope removal (with föhn drying on lee slopes), ITCZ-centered convective rain (the ITCZ shiftable ±13° via itczLat for the seasonal solves), convergence rain from smoothed wind divergence, and capacity overflow; a bimodal subtropical-subsidence drain (center shifted with itczLat) builds the desert belts, a winter-hemisphere storm-track term injects frontal rain in the ~30-45° band decaying with BFS distance-to-ocean (the Mediterranean-climate source), and evapotranspiration recycling (precipAccum × temp² capped) keeps warm wet interiors (Amazon/Congo) wet. Phase 3 percentile-normalizes atmospheric moisture and accumulated precip independently over land, blends them 50/50, caps by temperature, smooths, coastal-bumps, and upscales. earth_sim (worldgen.js) runs solveWind twice (season ±1) and solveMoisture twice (itczLat ±13), keeps a max-weighted blend of the two half-years as moisture, and uses the season-averaged wind to advect a separately-calibrated latitude/elevation temperature curve 60 steps (with ocean-current anomalies, westerly sweeps, and continentality layered on) — then applies a long list of explicit corrections, several of them named-region lat/lon Gaussians (Arabia, Patagonia, the Bengal foreland, the Andes LLJ, interior Asia) that patch what the solver got wrong; the two probe-confirmed sign bugs I found (seasonal monsoon hemisphere inverted; the physically-impossible all-easterly zonal mean) are plausibly the root causes several of those patches exist.

The real-wind path (realWindData.js) lazy-loads a JSON conversion of NCEP/NCAR 10m monthly climatology (tools/convert_wind_data.py, gaussian ~94×192 grid), exposing bilinear sampling in lat/lon with a +180° offset (the heightmap starts at the dateline) and fillRealWind, which is injected into generateWorld by WorldSim.jsx to avoid double-bundling the 2.3MB JSON into the worker. When enabled, one annual-mean field replaces BOTH seasonal solver winds (the seasonal ITCZ shift in the moisture pass alone recovers the monsoon rain belt), scaled m/s → internal units by 0.008 — but with the meridional axis un-flipped (finding 3), so the simulated-wind and real-wind paths currently disagree about which way v points. peopleSim/climate.js is a much later, runtime layer, unrelated to the solvers: every CLIMATE_INTERVAL (20) ticks it advances a mean-reverting random walk (_climIndex) plus rare exponentially-decaying volcanic shocks (_climShock), both drawn from the step-keyed passRng stream and persisted in saves (persist.js:104/166), and recomputes world.climMod — a per-tile fertility multiplier clamped to [0.70, 1.15] whose amplitude scales linearly from 0.1 at the equator to 0.4 at the poles, so harsh epochs correlate famine across whole latitude bands. territory.js multiplies harvestable fertility by climMod. This file is clean against both cardinal rules: the cadence is amortization not a content gate, volcanic winters are stochastic not scheduled, and nothing keys on the calendar.


## hydro-pipeline
_Files: src/sim/riverGen.js, src/sim/pipeline.js, src/sim/cropGen.js, src/sim/cropPackages.js, src/sim/resourceGen.js_

RIVER HYDROLOGY (riverGen.js): computeRivers(tw,th,tElev,tMoist,tTemp) runs a textbook priority-flood + D8 + flow-accumulation pipeline. Step 1 fills pits with a binary min-heap seeded from ocean tiles and the top/bottom rows (X wraps as a torus, so no left/right seeding), recording every raised tile in isRaised (= depression bed). Step 1c flood-fills the true ocean from the map edge and does exactly ONE 1-tile strait bridge, so single-pixel straits (Hormuz) join the ocean but chained gaps (Caspian via Kuma-Manych) stay inland — this is what keeps the Caspian terminal at fine resolution. Step 2 assigns D8 flow direction on the filled surface with a deterministic per-(tile,dir) hash jitter so rivers wander on flats without creating uphill flow (guaranteeing an acyclic graph). Step 3 computes per-tile runoff (moisture − evaporation + a cold×relief snowmelt term for exotic mountain-fed rivers) and accumulates it via Kahn topological sort; a `transmit` survival field (default 1) multiplies flow as it leaves each tile. Step 3b clusters raised tiles into basins, seals a basin's exits (flowDir=255) when its brim evaporative demand exceeds spill inflow, traces each tile's downstream fate into drainsTerminal (ocean-bound vs closed/inland-sea), applies transmission loss ONLY to terminal-draining arid/warm tiles, and re-accumulates. Step 4 classifies magnitude by ABSOLUTE km² catchment bars (converted to flow units via kmPerTile×avgRunoff, so resolution-invariant), with terminal drainage held to a 2.5× stricter bar, then a downstream-consistency pass forbids a river shrinking along its path. Step 5 validates candidate lake depressions against river inflow with a temperature-scaled evaporation multiplier.

PIPELINE (pipeline.js) is the second half of worldgen and the claimed single source of truth. buildTerritory downsamples the pixel world to a tile grid (RES), computes base climate fertility (tileFert), runs computeRivers, then applies a river moisture boost (widening each river into a floodplain band, recomputing tFert via the bell curve, adding an alluvial silt lift and a tFlood arid-floodplain mask), a lake moisture boost, geological modifiers (tropical laterite penalty, temperate chernozem bonus, volcanic-boundary bonus, coastal bonus), and Pass 3 which produces the analytical overlays tCrop (via cropGen.cropSuitability, with an arid-floodplain 0.92 override) and tCross (via peopleSim/transport.baseEdgeCost). It also runs generateAncestry (a full pre-history peopling Dijkstra that lays down a genetic-diversity substrate) and generateResources. I verified the single-source claim holds at the CODE level: worldGenWorker runs only generateWorld; buildTerritory runs exactly once on the main thread; peopleSimWorker, persist.js (load), and tools/_harness.mjs all consume the same buildTerritory/buildWorld outputs; and the people-sim reads only w.rivers.riverMag (grep-confirmed), which is the one river field shipped to the worker — so the worker and the main-thread fallback do not diverge. The important caveat is RESOLUTION: the app runs at 1920×960 while the tools run at 320–480 wide, and while the river classifier is resolution-invariant, floodplain width, lake sizing, and deposit radii are all in tile units, so probe measurements are not quantitatively the world the browser simulates.

CROPS (cropGen.js + cropPackages.js): cropSuitability computes a temperature×moisture bell, then envGate applies the shared environment gates (arid gate, discounted tropical laterite penalty using young-soil signals from plate-boundary distance/coast/river magnitude, elevation penalty, and a cold-gated river/coast alluvial pull toward 1). cropPackages adds five climate-envelope packages (wheat/rice/maize/sorghum/tubers) whose per-package suitability reuses the exact same envGate, so the continental east–west spread axis is emergent from climate rather than a diffusion constant. No time/era gating exists in any of these files — the resource `.era` and tech `.era` fields are cosmetic/tech-level labels, not time gates.

RESOURCES (resourceGen.js): 15 resources are placed either as per-tile regional fields (multi-octave value noise thresholded into provinces, gated by biome/elevation/moisture) or as scattered point mines (copper/tin/precious/gems) chosen by noise×sqrt(suitability) with a density fraction of candidate tiles becoming sites that radiate influence. Everything is deterministic from a hashed seed with no RNG state, and biome is derived from the (river/lake-boosted) tMoist/tTemp.


## settlement
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/settlement.js_

settlement.js is the per-settlement heart of the sim: one object per settlement created by makeSettlement (ids count up per-world via world._nextSettlementId; culture/name, deep ancestry mix, heritable org-aptitude, and crop packages are seeded at birth — ancestry blends parent stock with local stock weighted by residence density (tArrival) plus an asymmetric endemic-disease "settler barrier" from the malaria-signal gap between destination and parent home). Static site scores are cached once (waterAccess+riverAcc from a 3×3 scan, buildable area in a radius-14 box, confinement/Carneiro and ruggedness in small windows — all wrap x, clamp y). Each tick index.js rebuilds world._byId, then calls updateSettlement in fixed order: updateFood → updatePopulation (may kill it) → updateWealth → updateCoercedLabour → updateDevelopment → updateKnowledge → updateTier; after the loop urbanise() moves people toward hubs and aggregateFoodHierarchy computes next tick's _foodNet.

FOOD: land food = max(0, territory fertility sum − workedTiles×FARM_FERT_FLOOR) × FARM_YIELD_PER_FERT × techEff.farmYield × agriGate × armyLabor (only the conscript surge beyond ARMY_LABOR_FREE hurts the harvest) × _eraProd (emergent: ERA_PROD_BASE + SCALE·agri^POW·devGate on the COUNTRY capital's organisation; or the global anchor index) × a long multiplier stack — livestock (climate×domesticate-availability×tsetse), wet-tropic disease, arid burden, soil fatigue (updateSoil: a slow per-tile ratchet+heal every 600 ticks, hot/irrigated land salinises), tool-workability (forest needs metal axes, heavy soil the plough; river alluvium is exempt), irrigation (arid×river), alluvium (floodplain silt, half-share to coasts), minus cash-crop land. Pastoral herd calories are added after famine cuts as a floor; fish is a perishable local supplement that only matters where the sea is rich AND the land is poor. Supply seen by population = _foodNet (last tick's food-hierarchy redistribution) + fish; the granary integrates supply−demand (demand = pop×0.003×urbanFactor + army provisioning + slave rations) and clamps to a seasonality-scaled storage cap.

POPULATION & MOVEMENT: K = foodK (=supply/perCapita) under the default DISSOLVE_FARMS/locality model (housing then only drives the import-appetite via _housingPressed; legacy mode takes min(foodK, houseK) with a tier-0 rural cap that pushes surplus up to towns). Growth is a logistic Euler step scaled by _dt; empty granary switches to a 0.985^dt famine die-off; <1.5 people = abandoned, <8 people for 2000 history-ticks = withered. DISSOLVE splits people into rural/urban via ruralShare (falls with farm yield — the urban transition). urbanise() drifts a small fraction of each settlement toward its largest trade-reach neighbour (gap-scaled, unrest-boosted "refuge" flight, capped by destination K-room and by a farm-labour anchor that keeps ruralShare×K on the land), blending _orgApt pop-weighted. updateTier promotes/demotes among urban tiers with population-scaled bars (town and — under DISSOLVE — city bars scale with world population; the metropolis bar floats at 0.8× the largest city) and hysteresis.

ECONOMY & KNOWLEDGE: computeExportValue (memoised per tick by exportValueOf) prices what a settlement can sell — a fertility-scaled grain/wild base for farmers, raw materials, fish/shipping, horses, salt, plus manufactured legs from the shared craftLegs recipe (climate-gated textiles, ore-gated metalwork, size-scaled services, deterministic per-town idiosyncrasy), with an agglomeration lock-in that evolves _specKey/_specStr toward the comparative-advantage sector; army labour, sack penalty (linear 5000-tick recovery) and tech multiply the lot. updateWealth handles the only money sources/sinks here: coin-loss drain, optional bank credit, luxury budgets (computeLuxury), finite-reserve mining with seigniorage to the polity treasury. updateKnowledge grows six tracks under an emergent science multiplier (urban-core size, granary+flow surplus, trade contacts, rival-polity competition, heritable winter aptitude, ×_dt), with hard resource gates (metallurgy capped by reachable ore via oreTier — awareness diffuses freely but practice reads metalEff; navigation/mobility hard-gated), an org era-cap tied to the metal era, dark-age decay on population drawdown/isolation, KNOW_INTERVAL-staggered neighbour diffusion with the continental-axis bias applied only to agriculture, and crop diffusion/domestication. Overall the file honours both cardinal rules well: I found no year/era content gates (the one borderline case is an age-since-founding grace period, reported), and constants are mechanism-shaped with documented physical meaning; the significant problems are numerical (the unclamped logistic), persistence gaps, granularity-contract inconsistencies, and a tier-bar ordering flaw.


## conquest
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/conquest.js_

conquest.js is the POLITY PASS (run every T.POLITY_INTERVAL=150 steps from index.js). Each pass, rebuildCountries() regroups settlements by the persistent s.countryId into transient country views: capital = strongest urban member (settlementPower = people x military x org), hold reach c.range from the capital's transport techs (logistics-dominated under TECH_EFFECTS), res-scaled into c.holdReach so a 960-grid realm holds the same world-fraction as a 240-grid one, plus a liege hierarchy and nearest-city provinces. Persistent per-realm state (treasury, tax rate, debt, coin fineness, momentum, imperial-capacity stock, overlord link) lives on the polity record in entities.js, keyed by the founding-settlement id, and survives capital changes and save/load (persist.js serializes polities wholesale and the relevant settlement fields: loyalty, unrest, _homeland, _conqueredAt, _ambition, _serf...).\n\nThe core algorithm is a coercive-capacity budget. Capacity = CAP_K x (1 + capCoh x CAP_INST) x log2(1 + capPower/POW_REF) + loyal-seat bonuses, multiplied by a dominance term (capital power vs the era's MEDIAN capital power, ceiling itself emergent from cohesion tech — commit 72bb7eb) and a geographic-core term, floored by a slow-decaying imperial-hysteresis stock, then throttled by war duress (multi-front penalty, besieged/raided capital via world._fronts from armies.js), fiscal duress (last pass's army-pay solvency), both eased by org resilience, plus fast-decaying conquest MOMENTUM banked by armies.js per tile/storm. Each province's admin load = (euclidean + half the terrain surcharge from a capital-seeded Dijkstra over own-land/wilderness/sea only — rival land is impassable — + a construction-gated major-river toll) / holdReach, scaled by size, conquest recency, language friction, and divided by a military-coercion term (national army vs the province's garrison + identity-amplified rebel levy). Provinces are covered cheapest-first; over-budget ones bleed loyalty (slowed by org glue, capped over-rate so realms retreat ring-by-ring rather than imploding).\n\nSecession has five distinct paths: (1) shedFrontier — loyalty-collapsed seeds mark contiguous LOOSE tile patches (beyond the stress-shrunk grip) that touch the realm's frontier; shedPatch partitions each among the cities standing on it (town fallback; lone villages lapse stateless); (2) restoreNations — occupied homelands (recorded by recordOccupation, fading after HOMELAND_MEMORY) re-emerge as the SAME polity id, run before fresh successors both here and in (3) fragmentRealm — capital stormed (called from armies.js): treasury seized, realm ended, survivors split among the strongest spread-apart cities; (4) rebel — unrest (hunger/conscription/war fatigue/overtaxation/identity/serfdom, cooled by monuments legitimacy) boils over into destructive risings, rallying restless contiguous neighbours; the capital riots instead; (5) declareIndependence — an overmighty distant governor's ambition stock matures and he takes his province. All secessions are gated by contiguity (filterToConnectedBloc floods the parent's own land) and by hasOutsideBorder (fully-enclosed blocs fail and are ravaged instead), and all snap their claim + inherit reach via snapClaim.\n\nAfter the per-country loop: fiscal cycle (variable tax on wealth + FARM_RENT harvest levy -> treasury; court share to the capital; disburseTreasury pays army first — borrowing from the financier city, then debasing coinage — then deleverages, builds monuments, infrastructure, need-weighted relief); colonial dependencies (overlord link wired from sea.js, naval-reach-decayed investment/tribute/tech flows, independence when colony power outgrows projectable metropole force); balance of power (updateAlliances every ~600 steps: adjacency from the territory map, threat = strongest towering neighbour, coalitions of the co-threatened + trade partners, bloc might read by both armies.js war bars and — since commit 4e0d6de — the peaceful-absorption brake); absorbWeakNeighbors (probabilistic border-tile defection into dominant organised realms, gated by org tier caps, headroom incl. in-pass commitments, lopsided engulfment, cultural resistance, and seeded hash32 rolls — deterministic per seed); and eliminateEnclaves (full-map flood ceding sealed pockets to their surrounder, cities only when truly enclosed or vastly out-powered). I verified the cardinal rules hold: the identity 'era' weights key off world._civYear, which index.js derives from the leading capital's ORGANIZATION tech (civYearFromOrg), not the clock; all step arithmetic is either relative durations (grace, memory, supply windows) or amortization cadences. No named-region or outcome-fitted case detection exists in this file; the constants are numerous but each has a stated mechanism meaning. Overall the subsystem is coherent and unusually well-documented; the issues found are integration seams (enclave path skipping homeland bookkeeping, cadence coupling, resolution scaling of two constants) rather than architectural flaws.


## territory-identity
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/countryTerritory.js, src/sim/peopleSim/territory.js, src/sim/peopleSim/countryClaim.js, src/sim/peopleSim/crystallize.js, src/sim/peopleSim/identityField.js, src/sim/peopleSim/cohesion.js_

The political map is produced by a pipeline that runs every T.TERRITORY_INTERVAL ticks (index.js:200-210), in strict order: computeTerritory (territory.js) builds the ECONOMIC layer `world._territoryOwner` — a persistent per-settlement catchment: each settlement gets a sacred tier-scaled core block plus a guaranteed hinterland (nearest-wins, skipping conquered tiles stamped in `_tileCapturedAt`), then a multi-source Dijkstra claims remaining wilderness within a reach budget (TERRITORY_BASE + reachLevel·T.ORG_REACH), where the frontier-advance EFFORT is discounted by the two-field value model (÷(1+RIVER_REACH·river), ÷(1+VALUE_PULL·tileValue)) while the TRUE haul cost separately drives the food falloff; ownership persists across passes (neighbours' land is a wall), and tallyTerritory accumulates food/resources/borders. computeCountryTerritory (countryTerritory.js) then builds the POLITICAL layer `world._countryOwner`: per country it derives a reach budget from the political capital's tech (org·COUNTRY_REACH_ORG, ×logistics era boost gated by member count, ×size super-linear tail, ×personality, ×aptitude, eased by BUDGET_RAMP, res-scaled by tw/240), seeds a multi-source Dijkstra from every member settlement (basin scaled by √urban-pop and by recency of integration), with per-tile claim cost shaped by a soft construction-eased cap, fertility-hostility (ribbon realms), wet-tropic resistance, coherent value-noise meander, and optional power weighting (T.CLAIM_POW, default 0); water propagates cost but is never claimed. Four decoration passes follow: recolorByCapital (smooth capital cost-bisector recolouring of already-claimed land), fillEnclosedWaste (single-claimant pockets), closeRealmGaps (D=9 enclosed no-man's-land partition via 4-sweep enclosure test + 8-connected nearest-country flood), and smoothCountryBorders (majority filter, home tiles pinned).

The NEW persistent-territory feature (commits 8491fcb/8ac97d4/31942dc, default ON via T.PERSISTENT_TERRITORY=1) wraps that recompute: before rebuilding, last pass's `_countryOwner` is snapshotted into scratch `_coPrev`; after the decoration passes, mergePersistentTerritory (1) re-stamps every worked tile (`_territoryOwner`) with its settlement's countryId — the conquest ledger overrides the capital bisector, making heartlands path-dependent since countryId only changes by conquest/absorption/secession; (2) re-asserts as sticky marches any tile the fresh reach left wild (co<0) that a living country held last pass; (3) runs a 4-connected flood from the worked-core anchors and releases any claimed tile not reached through same-owner land (self-cleaning of marooned marches); then re-smooths. `_countryOwner`, `_countryClaim` and `_territoryOwner` are all serialised in persist.js, so the sticky map survives load. The rendered/authoritative-for-adoption border is a third layer: relaxClaim (countryClaim.js) crawls `_countryClaim` toward `_countryOwner` one res-scaled ring per CLAIM_RELAX_INTERVAL, with per-tile breakthrough pressure vs elevation+noise resistance, birth footholds (only a new realm's head settlement self-plants), instantaneous secession paint (`_claimSnap`), and an across-water snap for holdings on landmasses the crawl can't reach (exact landmass components via `landComp`). Crucially, despite its "render-only" header, `grownOwnerAt(claim)` is what adoptAndFound and crystallize use to decide which country a village belongs to — so the crawl IS sim-state; cities/sovereign seats keep their countryId, villages/towns follow the crawled land, developed stranded centres can found states, and nucleateFrontierStates mints new countries from developed stateless clusters (org ≥ T.ORG_STATE_MIN, fertility/disease/forest-iron-scaled population bars, capital-distance isolation).

Identity is a separate, genuinely render-only stack: identityField.js mirrors each settlement's top-4 culture/faith/language mixtures onto its `_territoryOwner` tiles (mirrorIdentityField, audited by a smoke-test invariant), then diffuseIdentityField floods town-county Voronois strictly inside each realm's `_countryOwner` land and blurs seams with an anchored vote stencil — nothing in the sim reads it (verified). cohesion.js turns identity mismatch into political force: per-axis mismatch between a province's mixture and the capital's dominant identity feeds unrest (identityGrievance), admin load (adminFriction), peaceful-absorption resistance and war casus belli (armies/conquest), weighted by era saliences from eraIdentityWeights(world._civYear) — and _civYear is legitimately emergent (index.js maps the LEADING capital's organisation through the CIV_ORG_YEAR anchor table, never the wall clock), though it is a single global scalar (see finding). Overall verdict: this subsystem is impressively mechanism-first — reach, hostility, noise, persistence and self-cleaning are all real mechanisms with physical meaning, resolution-invariance is handled deliberately, and determinism discipline (pass-seeded RNG, stable iteration, gather-then-write) is consistently good. The significant problems are one clear cardinal-rule time gate (crystallize's COVERAGE_RAMP), a cluster of save/load holes around unserialised integrator state (including a real field-name bug in persist.js), and three seams where the new persistence merge disagrees with the passes around it (cross-water release, core-vs-smoothing, 4-vs-8 connectivity).


## trade-network
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/roads.js, src/sim/peopleSim/transport.js, src/sim/peopleSim/sea.js, src/sim/peopleSim/spatialGrid.js_

Roads are not entities: the whole network is two per-tile Float32Arrays plus two sparse Set indexes. `world.roadQuality[ti]` (1.0 = no road; painted at 0.25, wearing toward 0.08 under traffic) is read by the shared edge-cost core so pathfinding prefers roads; `world.roadFlow[ti]` is a decaying (2%/tick) traffic EMA fed USAGE_PER_TRADE per active trade, driving both further paving (flow ≥ 0.2 paves toward QUALITY_MAX over ~5000 ticks) and abandonment (flow below 0.2 decays quality back to 1.0, at which point the tile leaves `_roadTiles` and the topology version bumps). Each settled settlement caches `s._tradeReach`, a Map of its ~12 nearest network peers found by a stamped typed-array Dijkstra (`computeReach`) that walks ONLY road tiles, navigable-river tiles (riverMag ≥ 3, cost 0.10 — the always-on river trade corridor), and settlement home tiles — giving transitive trade through chains. Reach is rebuilt in staggered slices over 120 ticks; network components (BFS over road+settlement tiles into `world._tileComp`) rebuild only when road version or settlement count changes. Road planning runs on a 240-tick cadence but evaluates exactly ONE settlement per tick from a snapshot queue with per-settlement exponential backoff: candidates in `partnerReachFor` range (pop + mobility/navigation-scaled) are ranked by a cheap benefit score (missing tier resources, export gap, food complementarity), then at most 2 unconnected + 1 connected peers get a real A* (`findPath`, stamped scratch, admissible 0.02/tile heuristic, distance²-bounded node budget); a build must clear a new-tile fraction bar (lower for bridging components, personality-modulated) and, for shortcuts, save ≥15% versus the network path, then is truncated at the first tile already in the destination's component. A separate close-neighbour pass paints Gabriel-graph links between towns within 20 tiles.

Trade iterates each settlement's merged reach (road ∪ sea, cheaper link wins) once every T.TRADE_STRIDE=3 ticks at stride× volume, deduping each unordered pair with an asymmetry-aware rule (lower id runs it unless it doesn't list the peer back). `runGeneralTradeBetween` runs two `sellGoods` legs (each side sells its exportValue × √minPop × T.TRADE_RATE, with Hume price-specie competitiveness and an FX rate from coin fineness); the buyer pays goods + freight (credited to the seller as carrying trade) + an 18% toll per intermediate settlement on the path (×4 for waterAccess chokepoints) + entrepôt brokerage + a tariff to a foreign buyer's state treasury — arithmetically conserved when all parties are alive. Sea links multiply volume by T.SEA_TRADE_MULT=8 (rising to ×2.5 that with steamship tech); river-flagged links (≥35% river tiles) get T.RIVER_TRADE_MULT=3.5. transport.js is the single cost core: three tile modes (land/river/water) with tech-parameterised costs cached per (knowledge-object, tick), a port tax on mode changes, an open-steppe riding discount, navigation-gated open water that eventually undercuts plains, and a road short-circuit — with `ignoreRoads` used by political-reach callers so borders follow terrain, not trade roads.

sea.js runs every 600 ticks: every nation-owning coastal settlement with a cached embark tile is a port; ONE multi-source Dijkstra flood over the ocean (wind-aligned costs, quadratic polar pack-ice ramp, deterministic per-world value-noise wobble, resolution-scaled budgets and visit cap) partitions water among ports; where two ports' waters meet, a direct lane forms iff its cost fits within ONE port's range (single-voyage rule), and a transitive closure over the port graph gives each sailing port its best T.SEA_MAX_PEERS=64 partners by gravity + luxury value discounted by freight — written symmetrically into `s._seaReach` (which, unlike road reach, is persisted across save/load to cover the 600-tick warm-up). Colonisation reuses the same flood: eligible ports collect continental shore tiles, pick either the most fertile clear site or — the spice quest — the reachable shore nearest an unreached luxury source, and launch a visible colony ship carrying people, coin, and a knowledge snapshot; on arrival it founds a self-governing dependency (own countryId, `_overlordCC` to the founder), or turns back with its people and coin if the site was taken mid-voyage. spatialGrid.js is a per-step-rebuilt 16-tile bucket grid with longitude-wrapping radius queries (whole-row dedup when the window spans the globe, stale dead entries filtered at visit time) — I traced its wrap, clamp, and coverage math and found it correct. On the assignment's specific question: commit 47c62bc's river-highway REACH lever is confirmed present in territory.js (`eff /= (1 + T.RIVER_REACH)` on riverMag ≥ 2 tiles) and confirmed inert by default (tuning.js RIVER_REACH def: 0) — distinct from the river trade corridor in roads.js, which IS on by default. Cardinal-rule audit of all four files: clean — every `world.step` use is cadence/backoff/cooldown (rates, not calendar gates), and constants are physical parameters (freight ratios, toll rates, ice latitudes) with no named-region or fitted-outcome code.


## economy
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/money.js, src/sim/peopleSim/inflation.js, src/sim/peopleSim/tech.js, src/sim/peopleSim/agriculture.js, src/sim/peopleSim/foodHierarchy.js, src/sim/peopleSim/habitability.js, src/sim/peopleSim/slavery.js_

MONEY. Coin is (nearly) a closed system. The single faucet is mining (settlement.js updateWealth): finite per-tile depositReserve is drawn down by a rate ∝ richness × √pop × organisation × (1 + MINE_COERCE·unfree-ratio); the realm's mint takes SEIGNIORAGE_RATE off the top into the polity treasury and the miner keeps the rest. The drain is the per-tick specie loss `wealth -= wealth * COIN_LOSS_RATE * dt` (the README's "wear drain"). Every transfer I audited is arithmetically conserved: the food hierarchy moves pay from buyer to seller symmetrically, capped by a pre-sweep spare-coin snapshot so a node can never spend coin it earns later in the same sweep; the slave market clears buyers and sellers pro-rata against the same `traded` quantity (probe confirms Σ paid = Σ received to fp noise); taxes/tribute/relief/court-share in conquest.js all move coin between settlement purses and polity treasuries without minting. The one deliberate second faucet is the experimental credit layer (T.CREDIT_RATE, default 0): banking-organisation hubs mint tracked credit up to CREDIT_MAX_MULT× specie and call it back on collapse. money.js itself is a pure display ledger: recordIn/recordOut accumulate per-tick flows into 14 income / 12 spending channels, folded once per tick into a 0.95-decay EMA for the info panel — it never moves coin. INFLATION is quantity-theory per road-network component (roads.js's _networkComponents): every 50 ticks M = Σ settlement wealth and T = Σ max(1, exportValue·√pop) per component; a baseline REF = global M/T is locked ONCE (currently gated on step≥5000 + ≥3 components — see findings), and each component's price P EMAs toward a log-saturating curve of (M/T)/REF, clamped [0.4, 3.0], with a wider linear _inflRaw twin for the wheat-price ticker. Crucially, the only mechanic that actually reads P is the Hume price-specie-flow term in roads.js (dear regions export less, reciprocally, so specie self-distributes); grain deliberately trades in real terms, and the advertised wage/building-cost effects don't exist (finding).

TECH & ERAS. The six continuous knowledge tracks (agriculture, construction, organization, metallurgy, navigation, mobility — grown in settlement.js by practice and contact diffusion) are pure research currency; tech.js converts them into an 82-node Civ-style DAG. techState() runs a fixed-point sweep: a tech is discovered the instant all prereq techs are held AND its req(k) thresholds are met — no research economy, no randomness, and (verified by probe) full knowledge unlocks all 82 nodes. The "era" is strictly derived — the max era among discovered techs — and a grep confirms it is only ever READ for display/chronicle (HUD ribbon, era.reached events, world._eraAt calendar labels); nothing gates on it, satisfying cardinal rule 1. techEffects() sums per-tech bonus channels (farm/fish/military/build/reach/cohesion/defense/trade/wealth/sea/logistics plus ability booleans), grants imminent techs 60% partial credit scaled by progress so the staircase hugs the old smooth curves, normalises the "level" channels by their full-tree totals, and lerps against the legacy continuous formulas by `blend` — probe-verified byte-exact at blend 0 except logisticsLevel, which is tech-driven at any blend by design. Results are memoised on the exact six-track+blend tuple in a bounded, clear-on-full cache (pure function, so determinism-safe).

AGRICULTURE & HABITABILITY. A settlement's land food is gated by agriGate = forager floor + (1−floor)·dev, where dev = min(1, effective-agriculture / AGRI_FULL_AT) and effective agriculture is the knowledge track capped by a static per-tile domestication CEILING: a once-computed flood-fill of landmass sizes gives ceil = floor + (1−floor)·√(size/largest) (Diamond's isolated-continent thesis), times a wet-tropic penalty (t>0.78, m>0.6). With the experimental CROP_AXIS lever the cap further intersects the best owned crop package's suitability × storability at the home tile. habitability.js supplies three pure climate-state brakes — malaria, tsetse, and rain-fed aridity (river-escapable) — carefully calibrated to the same Holdridge effective-moisture scale as the biome classifier; settleHostility feeds settlement spacing, claimHostility feeds political reach costs. All of it is state-only, no time inputs — clean under both cardinal rules.

FOOD HIERARCHY & COERCED LABOUR. foodHierarchy.js runs every tick over the liege tree: prices are a fixed per-tier schedule (2/8/14/22), then one post-order sweep per root has each node BUY its children's offers (offer = pool × tier ship-fraction × exp(−d/range) haul survival, where range grows with the destination tier, the shipper's construction/mobility tech with an industrial leap past 0.85, and a river/coast water-corridor bonus), capped strictly by the buyer's snapshotted spare coin — grain up, coin down, both exactly conserved; what isn't bought stays rural and _foodNet feeds next tick's updateFood. Coerced labour follows docs/coerced-labor.md steps 1–2 faithfully in structure: updateCoercedLabour (per tick) sets a wealth-, food-security- and suitability-gated target for _unfree, consumes local _captives first, applies harshness-scaled attrition, rolls a deterministic seeded-hash revolt above a 60% unfree ratio, drifts _cashFrac toward a labour-backed cash-crop allocation whose output folds into luxury supply, and posts residual demand; slavery.js (every 50·G ticks) has strong settlements raid ≥2×-weaker foreign neighbours within 28 tiles into _captives, then clears a single GLOBAL market: buyers pro-rata by coin-capped demand, sellers pro-rata by stock, at a flat price of 8 — people moved, coin moved, nothing minted. The doc's demand sinks (cash crops via _cashFrac and luxury income, mining via coerceMine, feeding via SLAVE_FEED, exclusion from settlementPower and the tax base, revolt) are all genuinely wired. The gaps are in the raid supply side and captive-stock physics (see findings). Overall verdict: this slice of the codebase is unusually disciplined — conservation is real where it claims to be, the era system is genuinely emergent, and the problems found are at the persistence boundary, one lone step-gate, and granularity/limbo details rather than in the core arithmetic.


## identity-culture
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/cultures.js, src/sim/peopleSim/faiths.js, src/sim/peopleSim/dynasties.js, src/sim/peopleSim/personality.js, src/sim/language.js_

The identity layer is three parallel population MIXTURES per settlement plus a genealogy. Every settlement carries `culMix` (peoples), `langMix` (spoken tongues), and `faithMix` (creeds) as top-4 [[id,share]] lists kept normalized by a shared normalizeMix (sort desc, fold tail past MIX_K into the dominant, renormalize; faiths additionally pop shares < 5%). All three passes run on _ivl-stretched intervals (150 ticks × SIM_GRANULARITY; dynasties 25), with duration thresholds divided by world._dt so per-history rates stay calibrated — the cadence is pure amortization, never a content gate. cultures.js moves two layers at different speeds: language standardizes toward the realm's prestige tongue (the capital's, or a 'national standard' branched once the realm has ≥4 members and org ≥ 0.25) at ASSIM_RATE scaled by organization and a transport-distance falloff read from conquest.js's `_capCost` stamps; ethnic identity follows at 10% of that rate and only in proportion to how far speech has already shifted. New peoples are born four ways, all state-gated: isolation (no same-culture trade/sea peer for DIVERGE_AFTER), distance drift (beyond a cohesion radius that grows with organization+mobility+construction as a fraction of map width — stone age fragments, tech consolidates), political ethnogenesis (a language-shifted bloc under a durable non-core state), and crystallization-time branching. Each daughter gets a language via branchLanguage (copy parent, drift 1+d·8 sound changes) and names via a per-culture nameCounter into hash-keyed word streams — fully deterministic given call order, which is protected by id-sorted iteration and per-entity/per-pass RNG substreams (rng.js: sfc32 seeded from hash32(seed, system, id|step); no serialized RNG state).

faiths.js models the full lifecycle: lazy folk faiths per lineage anchor (folkAnchorOf walks up until divergence ≥ 0.5 — live for crystallize's near-branches at divergence 0.15+, though every divergence cultures.js itself uses is ≥ 0.5, so those daughters always grow their own gods); organized genesis gated on organization ≥ 0.26, pop ≥ 450, urban stress, and per-family saturation (an emergent 'axial age' with no clock anywhere); spread along trade/sea/proximity graphs with a winner-take-all pull per settlement combining zeal, follower-count network effect, state pressure, in-group conformity, and a doctrine↔personality affinity dot product; state adoption from the capital's dominant faith with loyalty coupling (LOYAL_MATCH/LOYAL_CLASH) and faithShapePersonality feedback; bounded schism (per-root branch cap, cooldowns, distance from the see, war/succession-crisis triggers), rare syncretism, and a cull of stateless faiths with <2 dominant settlements. Pilgrimage transfers (never mints) a wealth sliver from the faithful to each faith's origin see. Doctrine is six 0–1 axes rolled at founding, biased toward the founding realm's temperament, mutated at schism, and drifted by lived war/peace.

dynasties.js samples a real kin graph around each throne once the capital crosses LITERACY_MIN (org 0.22): rulers marry (25% via a cross-realm royal court), breed with vigor-scaled annualized hazards, and every person is dealt a lifespan ONCE at birth from a development-softened distribution (mortF from capital org — medicine is emergent). Ages and reigns are measured on the dynYear clock (calendar.js: uniform 0.25 yr/step) — importantly, this is duration arithmetic only (age = now − born), and the era-anchored displayYear is never read by any mechanic, so ruler aging is NOT a rule-1 violation; the clock is a fixed linear transform of step, exactly like the tick-duration thresholds used everywhere else. Governance form (monarchy/theocracy/republic/despotism/elective) derives with hysteresis from personality, faith doctrine, and capital primacy; succession runs claim-ranked primogeniture with representation over the whole house (heirByLaw's DFS with ancestor climb), falling to regencies, bastards, and finally a genuine succession crisis (legitimacy shatters, loyalty/unrest shocks, `_crisisAt` read by the war and schism systems). A slow `_dynLegit` accumulator (house tenure + reign length + adult-heir presence) feeds unrest relief and handover smoothness. personality.js is the cleanest file: seeded intrinsic −1..1 anchors (80%) plus a weak material prior (20%), gentle mean-reverting event drift, faith shaping as a bounded reversible offset, and pivot-centred behaviour multipliers consumed by conquest/roads/sea. Save/load serializes cultures/languages/faiths/persons/dynasties Maps and polity records verbatim; determinism holds well overall, with the specific persistence and seed-collision gaps reported in findings. Both cardinal rules are respected in this subsystem — every gate I traced (axial window, literacy, cohesion radius, theocracy/republic derivation) reads world state, and I found no named-outcome fitting; the findings are mechanism bugs, not scripting.


## narrative
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/events.js, src/sim/peopleSim/entities.js, src/sim/peopleSim/historiography.js, src/sim/peopleSim/chronicle.js, src/sim/calendar.js_

History in Simman is a single append-only structured log plus views. Every consequential moment is recorded by logEvent (src/sim/peopleSim/events.js) as {id, step, type, ...fields}: actors as entity ids, display names captured at event time (so renames never rewrite the past), places as x/y. ev.id IS the array position, and world._evIndex maps entity keys ("p:7", "s:12", "c:3", "f:2", "d:1") to id lists so per-entity history is O(own events); reindexEvents rebuilds the index after load, and a 200k-cap/150k-keep compactor bounds memory on pathological runs (measured rate ~0.045 events/step, so it essentially never fires). Prose is generated at READ time only: NARRATE templates render each event per viewing side (a capture is "Lost X" in one ledger, "Captured X" in the other), categoryOf assigns panel colors, and condenseChronicle collapses same-step war-declaration bursts. getChronicle (chronicle.js) is the omniscient per-realm view: it just maps eventsFor("p:"+id) through narrate. chronicleTick (every 300 steps, from index.js) is the milestone detector — it walks world.countries and logs slow-drift events (era reached, city-count peaks, wealth/debt power-of-two bands, serfdom/emancipation, settlement archetypes via a per-settlement _chronFlags bitmask), with milestone memory on the persistent polity record (p.chron). It also appends world._eraAt — the step the leading civ first reached each tech era — which is the display calendar's only input.

The entity layer (entities.js) gives polities a persistent lifecycle independent of world.countries (a per-pass aggregation view rebuilt from settlements each polity pass). A polity record holds treasury/fiscal state, personality, chronicle memory and culture/faith/dynasty attachments; records are never deleted — endPolity sets endedStep, and ensurePolity's re-open path is how "restored Poland IS Poland." reconcilePolities, run at the end of updatePolities, is meant to be the registrar (register substantial newcomers with a polity.founded event, close vanished realms with polity.ended) — but in practice the founding gate is defeated by earlier silent ensurePolity calls from personality.js and chronicleTick (see findings; empirically only cradle foundings ever log). Historiography (historiography.js) then renders history as a given realm's scribes would keep it, via three deterministic transforms keyed on hash32(seed, viewer, eventId, salt): an information horizon (foreign events survive with probability decaying by distance from the capital, boosted by its organization tech; the marginal band renders as rumor; seven "TRAVELS" types propagate worldwide), archive loss (each sack of the viewer's capital — polity.shattered events — burns pre-sack records with 45% survival each; a burned founding is retold as an invented myth stretched up to 45% older), and court bias (biasedNarrate rewrites the viewer's conquests as liberations, losses as treachery; shame-omission drops up to 40% of non-capital defeats scaled by aggression). exportHistory bundles the true log plus each major realm's biased tradition. Everything round-trips through persist.js (events array + polities Map serialized whole; _evIndex and countries rebuilt).

The calendar (src/sim/calendar.js) is three clocks. stepToYear is the plain linear label; dynYear is a second uniform clock at half rate used by the dynasty layer purely for durations (ages, reign lengths); displayYear is THE displayed year, piecewise-linearly pinned to the historical date of each emergent era via world._eraAt, with a floor keeping boundaries strictly increasing and a saturating ease inside the open leading era (a world stuck in bronze creeps toward 700 BC and never shows AD). I verified rule 1 holds in code: displayYear/eraAt are consumed only by WorldSim.jsx and the worker snapshot (read-only labels); the demographic anchor reads world._civYear derived from emergent organization, not the clock; dynasties consume only relative dynYear spans. I probed displayYear/displayStep numerically: strictly monotone, exact inverse round-trip, and correct on stalled and leapfrogged era timelines. The genuine problems in this subsystem are concentrated in entity-lifecycle integrity (silent registration/re-open paths gutting the founded/ended/restored event stream — probe: 10 of 13 registry entries have no founding, 6 of 7 ended ids were never founded), narrator coverage gaps, and horizon transforms that don't apply to coordinate-less events; the calendar and the event-log core are solid, with only stale comments and a compaction/id-stability wrinkle.


## war-shocks
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/armies.js, src/sim/peopleSim/shocks.js_

ARMIES (armies.js). Two periodic passes. musterArmies (every MUSTER_INTERVAL) grows each settled garrison (s.army) toward a cap = people × armyCapFrac, where armyCapFrac is a tier fraction, +capital bonus, ×personality aggression, ×org-scaling, ×global dial. It is gated by FOOD (an uncovered food-deficit desert-melts the army to ARMY_DESERT) and by SOLVENCY (an insolvent treasury melts it to BANKRUPT_DESERT × shortfall). In war (c._warStamp within CONSCRIPT_WINDOW) a temporary conscript levy is added on top, drawn from the rural populace and scaled by defensive load + offensive fronts × war-commitment, mustering MOBILIZE_SPEED× faster. A national MANPOWER pool (regenerating fraction of national pop, drained by battle deaths) hard-caps the summed standing army. advanceFronts (every CONQUEST_INTERVAL) is the war engine: it scans the territory-owner grid, and for each owned tile finds the strongest adjacent enemy settlement that clears a state-derived attack bar (base ATTACK_MIN_RATIO × thin-tile defensibility × terrain-defence(river/mountain/construction) × trade-peace × war-exhaustion bar × casus-belli × dominance × coalition-balance × truce check). Qualifying tiles group into attacker→defender 'pairs'; a pair whose front reaches the defender core (coreRadius+ASSAULT_MARGIN) is flagged canStorm. War is then decided not by local garrisons but by CONSERVED national field armies: each realm's natMight (Σ garrison×tech, capped by manpower/exhaustion) is split by priority weights across all its fronts (defence 2.5× priority, concentration falloff on offence), shares summing to one, so a realm stretched thin fights weakly everywhere. Per front, adv = attForce/(defForce×encircleMul) drives tile capture (budget from adv-1, blitzed by dominance); at the core a siege grinds the garrison (SIEGE_DMG) against homeMight (max of garrison and morale-weighted citizen militia floor) until it breaks (SIEGE_BREAK), then the city flips, momentum banks, captives are taken, a capital storm calls fragmentRealm, and knowledge decays (dark-age). Attrition drains both garrisons every pass; the net garrison shrinkage is charged back to each realm's manpower pool at pass end.

SHOCKS (shocks.js). updateShocks runs every tick. Four state-clock cadences (step % CHECK) drive it: endemic disease-load update, first-contact scan, famine roll, plague roll. Famine: on a due tick with probability FAMINE_CHANCE×_dt, pick a settlement seed and strike every settlement within FAMINE_RADIUS with _harvestMul=FAMINE_SEVERITY until a randomized end-step; settlement.js applies _harvestMul to land food ONLY while step < _famineUntil (correctly gated — no lingering famine), and shockUnrest/faiths add grievance during the window. Plague: a population/port-weighted seed is infect()ed; infect sets _plagueUntil (PLAGUE_DUR/_dt), marks it active, adds to world._plagued, and logs one wave event per realm. Each tick the lifecycle loop kills people (PLAGUE_MORT ×urban-crowding ×virgin ×_dt), spreads along trade+sea reach (PLAGUE_SPREAD, sea ×2) via per-peer rng rolls, and burns out into a PLAGUE_IMMUNE window. The endemic-load system diffuses each settlement's disease load toward max(local target from density/livestock/connectivity, DIFFUSE×best trade/sea peer), so a connected landmass converges high; contactEpidemic detects a first sea contact across a CONTACT_GAP immunity gap and sweeps the low-immunity pool around it with a 6× virgin-soil epidemic (the Columbian collapse). All durations/rates are _dt-scaled so history-time behaviour is granularity-invariant.

TIME-GATING VERDICT: I found no time/era content gates in either file. Every CHECK/INTERVAL is a step%N amortization cadence (explicitly allowed), and every spawn probability is multiplied by _dt so the per-history-time rate is invariant to SIM_GRANULARITY. Shock magnitude/spread is driven by emergent state (populations, trade/sea graphs, disease-load gaps, national armies, tech-scaled might), and war qualification is gated on emergent power ratios, exhaustion, coalitions and casus-belli — never on the calendar. The one weakness against the spirit of the rules is that plague lethality and famine severity are flat constants that do not soften with emergent medical/agricultural development (finding 2), so late-history shocks stay antiquity-strength.


## core-loop
_Files: src/sim/peopleSim/index.js, src/sim/peopleSim/state.js, src/sim/peopleSim/tuning.js, src/sim/peopleSim/rng.js, src/sim/peopleSim/invariants.js_

**Tick orchestration (index.js).** `stepPeopleSim` runs a fixed pass order per tick: (1) rebuild the reused `world._byId` Map and tally live population/capacity; (2) demographic anchor — if `T.ANCHOR_POP>0`, an integral controller nudges `world._eraProd` (±2%/tick slew, anti-windup capped at 4× pop lead, clamped 0.5–240) so world population tracks a log-interpolated historical curve keyed on `world._civYear`, the pseudo-year derived from the leading capital's *organisation* via the CIV_ORG_YEAR table (explicitly de-pinned from the wall clock; one-tick lag acknowledged); with the anchor off (the actual default) `world._eraProd` is held at 1 and each settlement computes its own emergent `s._eraProd = ERA_PROD_BASE + SCALE·agri^POW·devGate` in settlement.js; (3) climate overlay (every 20 ticks) before territory so food tallies feel it; (4) territory/country-Voronoi/adopt/nucleate (every `T.TERRITORY_INTERVAL`), border crawl every 12 ticks; (5) per-settlement update, urbanise, food hierarchy (produces `_foodNet` consumed next tick); (6) shocks, crystallise, roads, inflation-then-trade; (7) `pruneDead` every 32 ticks mid-loop; (8) military/sea/soil/polity/culture/faith/pilgrimage/slavery/dynasty passes on intervals; (9) money folding and the opt-in invariant pass. `T.SIM_GRANULARITY` (G) sets `world._dt = 1/G`: per-tick clocks scale their rates by `_dt`, "rate" passes stretch their interval via `_ivl(base)=round(base·G)`, and "recompute" passes deliberately keep their interval. The classification is mostly right (shocks, learning, migration, ship movement all consume `_dt`; conquest/polity/muster/culture/faith/slave/dynasty stretch) but climate and soil are misclassified — see findings.

**World state (state.js).** `createWorld` downsamples worldgen to TILE_RES=2 tile arrays (elev/temp/moist/fert/coast/tFlood/deposits/wind/riverMag/ancestry). Fertility is max-pooled over the 2×2 block (so thin river-valley ribbons survive), the floodplain mask is block-OR'd from the pipeline's mask (with a legacy fert>0.85 ∧ moist<0.30 fallback), and flood tiles get `moist = max(moist, 0.45)` so the transport core doesn't price the valley as desert — a mutation with an unintended side effect on the disease signals (finding 1). Cradle seeding is either algorithmic (score = fertility + river bonus + Carneiro land-circumscription − sea-fraction penalty, greedy top-10 with 60-tile separation) or, on earth presets with `EARTH_HEARTHS=1` (default), three hard-coded historical hearth coordinates. There is deliberately no shared `world.rng` — randomness is drawn per system.

**Tuning and RNG.** tuning.js is a 213-lever schema flattened into the live mutable `T`; every module reads `T.KEY` at use-time so sliders apply next pass, and `TUNING_VERSION` invalidates derived caches. I verified consumption of all 213 keys by grep: 212 are read somewhere in src/sim; only `WAR_DEF_SPLIT` is dead (finding 3). `applyTuning` filters to known keys and finite numbers but does not clamp to schema min/max (finding 4). rng.js partitions randomness into stateless substreams: `passRng(seed, name, step)` and `entityRng(seed, name, id)`, hashed by FNV-1a into a splitmix32-expanded sfc32. I extracted every substream name in the codebase: pass names {climate, contact, crystallize, dynasty, religion, settlers, settlers.site, shocks, urban} and entity names {doctrine, personality, schism, syncretism, *.cul} are disjoint, so there is currently **no cross-bleed** — though nothing structurally prevents a future name collision between the two families (a pass at step N would replay entity id N's stream exactly), and the 32-bit hash means rare whole-stream collisions across steps are statistically expected but practically harmless. Streams being stateless-per-derivation also means saves need no RNG state, which persist.js exploits.

**Invariants (invariants.js).** Opt-in (`world._checkInvariants`), watch-only: per settlement it checks finiteness of people/wealth/food/army, negative people/wealth, tier ∈ [0,3], six knowledge tracks ∈ [0,1], loyalty/unrest ∈ [0,1]; it publishes `totalCoin` (settlement wealth + live polity treasuries from `world.polities`, the source of truth that `world.countries` `_treasury` mirrors) and `totalPeople` on `world.debug` for conservation watching, with a 256-step per-key warn throttle in a module-global Map cleared by `resetInvariantState` at world creation. Coverage is decent for the "NaN crept in" class but has gaps around in-transit ships, negative army/food, and multi-world processes (findings/minors). Overall verdict: the orchestration layer is thoughtfully built and unusually well-documented; the real defects are concentrated in cross-cutting seams (granularity classification, the floodplain moisture mutation, unclamped tuning ingress) rather than in the core loop.


## persistence-workers
_Files: src/sim/persist.js, src/peopleSimWorker.js, src/worldGenWorker.js_

Persistence (src/sim/persist.js) stores a world as identity + dynamics. Identity is the meta block (W/H/seed/preset/oceanLevel/tecParams); on load, pipelineBuild re-runs the deterministic worldgen pipeline from that meta, then initPeopleSim creates a fresh sim world (whose freshly-seeded cradles and events are immediately discarded) and the dynamic state is laid over it. Dynamics are: a per-settlement whitelist (SETT_FIELDS, ~60 scalar fields plus pos and a cloned knowledge object), the polity/culture/language/faith/person/dynasty registries serialized verbatim as Map entries (all plain-JSON records — I verified none contain Maps/Sets or ±Infinity), the raw event log (reindexed on load), ships, seven id counters, non-default tuning overrides (applied before initPeopleSim so granularity levers shape createWorld), six big per-tile typed arrays base64-encoded (roadQuality, roadFlow, countryClaim, countryOwner, territoryOwner, capturedAt), finite mine reserves, five world tables (_warExhaust, _linkMoney, _inflRaw, _manpower, _plagueEvAt, _plagued), per-settlement _seaReach, and the climate walk (_climIndex/_climShock) + demographic anchor (_eraProd/_popTotal). Everything else is declared a per-pass transient that \"re-warms\" on its own cadence; loadWorld warms only computeTerritory before returning. Version handling is a hard equality check on SAVE_VERSION=1 with no migration framework. hashWorld is a weak FNV-style digest over roughly the saved scalar subset, used by tools/smoke.mjs to assert save→load hash identity.

peopleSimWorker.js owns the live sim off the main thread. Messages in: init/load (build or restore the world), control (play/speed), select/selectRealm/view/chronicle-mode/dynasty-open (what detail to ship), tune, save/export-history (post JSON back), and editor.placeCountry (synchronously seeds a full realm then bursts 640 steps so it settles, including one sea pass). The step loop is a MessageChannel-yielding scheduler: paced modes accumulate fractional ticks from wall time (clamped to 250 ms gaps) and run them under a 12 ms budget per slice; a speed ≥ 100000 sentinel busy-loops. Snapshots post ~30/s regardless of sim speed: a light record per settled settlement, per-country aggregates read from world.countries, full detail for the selected settlement only, a chronicle re-narrated only when its signature changes, an incremental event feed (last-40 cap), and the big per-tile arrays — roadFlow every frame, owner/roadQuality/countryClaim/identity-field/seaLanes/tileComp on a 1-in-6 \"static\" cadence — moved zero-copy via transferables after being sliced and cleaned (ghost-owner tiles of dead settlements are masked; countryClaim is overlaid with worked-territory country ids). The main thread (WorldSim.jsx applySnapshot) maintains a mirror world object: replaces settlements/countries/_byId every snapshot, keeps last-known static arrays between sends, and renders from it. worldGenWorker.js is trivial by comparison: one generate message → generateWorld → post result with all top-level typed arrays transferred (nested rivers/deposits arrays are structure-cloned), errors falling back to main-thread generation.

Overall verdict for my dimension: the worker protocol is solid — the pacing, transferable hygiene, static-cadence gating and mirror bookkeeping are careful and I found no true races or lost-message paths (messages queue while the burst loop yields; the scheduled/playing flags are consistent). The weak spot is save COMPLETENESS: the whitelist architecture (SETT_FIELDS + hand-picked world tables) has drifted from the sim, and the smoke test's hash covers only the whitelisted fields, so drift is structurally invisible. I traced six pieces of genuinely persistent, behavior-bearing state that do not round-trip (capture clocks, river/site statics + heritable aptitude, soil memory, truces, the inflation baseline, the reach ramp) — each silently and permanently changes a loaded world relative to an uninterrupted run, well beyond the header's acknowledged warm-up transients. One protocol-level meta bug (genMeta/tecParams taken from the UI instead of the save file) can corrupt re-saved files. No cardinal-rule violations in these files: the worker's time constants are cadence/pacing only, and persist stores state rather than gating on it.


## ui-render
_Files: src/WorldSim.jsx_

The renderer is a single 1920×960 canvas composited every frame by draw() (src/WorldSim.jsx:1373). All map state arrives as read-only mirrors: worldRef (raw worldgen arrays), terRef (buildTerritory output — tCrop/tMoist/rivers/deposits at the same 1920×960 grid since RES=1), and peopleRef (a snapshot mirror fed ~30Hz by the peopleSim worker via applySnapshot:2439, which also triggers draw()). Pan/zoom lives in refs (viewX/Y/ZRef) applied as a ctx transform each frame (1386-1395) so all drawing stays in fixed canvas coordinates; hit-testing inverts it in screenToCanvas. A hard-coded useMercator=false means every projection helper (screenYtoDataY/dataYtoScreenY) is currently an identity clamp.

Each draw builds or blits a BASE raster per view, then stacks overlays. Views listed in BASE_CACHE_VIEWS (terrain, dev lenses, resources, country, atlas) are pure functions of the world, cached to an offscreen canvas keyed by a string of view+seed+toggles (1409-1411); money/roads add a step-bucket tag so they refresh every 8 sim-steps. Non-cached views (the identity lenses culture/faith/language/ancestry, plus society) rebuild their per-pixel base every draw and route it through the SAME offscreen canvas as a putImageData shim so the pan/zoom transform applies (1666-1673). On top of the base sits the peopleSim overlay, itself cached to a second offscreen canvas (psOverlayRef) regenerated every 30 sim-steps or on layer-toggle change (1892-2271): country claims (assignCountryColors runs a hue-wheel repulsion so neighbours stay distinct, seeded by previous hues for smooth drift), colony stripes (stripeCells clips 45° lines to colony tiles), province seams, roads by flow, and for identity lenses a bounded multi-source BFS (identityFillRef) that floods each settlement's tier-sized disk so peoples/faiths/tongues paint contiguous regions; ancestry adds a 10s wall-clock "peopling replay" that forces per-frame overlay rebuilds while active. Settlement glyphs (tier shapes, wealth fill, garrison/shock rings, capital stars), sea lanes, colony ships, wind particles and money-coin particles (bucketed into 4 brightness batches, positions reused across frames) draw directly on the main ctx every frame.

Three loops drive redraws: worker snapshots (30Hz while playing), a rAF animLoop that redraws only for per-frame-motion views (wind, money, ancestry replay), and a main-thread fallback rAF sim loop used only if the worker fails. React state is deliberately kept out of the hot path — everything draw() needs is mirrored into refs — but setLiveStep(snap.step) still re-renders the whole 4,187-line component at snapshot rate so the year counter ticks. The globe path resamples the base ImageData into a 4096×2048 RGB texture (throttled to 4Hz, one reused 25MB buffer) for GlobeView. The rendering layer is clean on both cardinal rules: the era/year in the top bar are derived read-only labels (psStats.leadingEra, displayYearStr), no mechanic reads them, and the only constants are presentation ones. Overall the architecture is sound and heavily perf-conscious; the real defects are concentrated in the base-layer cache-key mechanism, which is subtly broken in three distinct ways.


## ui-state
_Files: src/WorldSim.jsx_

WorldSim.jsx is one 4187-line component that owns the whole app shell. World creation flows: seed/preset state → `useEffect([seed])` → `generate()` → a WorldGenWorker (terminated-and-replaced per run; falls back to main-thread generateWorld on worker error) → `finalizeWorld(w)`, which aliases `w.seed`, runs `buildTerritory` on the main thread (terRef), then tears down and re-creates the PeopleSimWorker. The sim worker gets either an `init` (a slim clone of only the fields createWorld reads) or a `load` (pending save JSON stashed in pendingSaveRef by the hidden file input), plus catch-up messages for control/view/tune. The worker self-paces ticks against wall-clock (a fractional accumulator; a MessageChannel busy-loop for "Max") and posts ~30Hz snapshots; `applySnapshot` writes them into a mirror object in `peopleRef.current` shaped like the real sim world, keeping "static" arrays (owner, roadQuality, countryClaim, sea lanes, cultures/faiths/languages, identity fields) from the last static send, accumulating an event `_feed` (cap 250) and a psHistoryRef time series (sampled every 100 sim-steps, cap 5000), then calls draw(). If the worker fails, a main-thread fallback sim steps inside a time-budgeted rAF loop instead (8ms/frame budget, draw every 3rd batch) — but many worker-only features (select detail, chronicle, dynasty, editor placement, perspective toggle) silently degrade there.

Rendering is aggressively ref-based to avoid re-render churn: everything the per-frame paths need (viewRef, layersRef, selectedSettlementIdRef, editorArmedRef, pan/zoom viewX/Y/ZRefs, hover throttle) is mirrored from state into refs by small effects, and `draw()` — a useCallback with stable deps — reads only refs. draw() composes: a base raster (per-view pixel loops into a reused ImageData) cached to an offscreen canvas keyed by view|seed|toggles (BASE_CACHE_VIEWS blit every frame; money/roads add a step-bucket to the key), the political/identity overlay rendered to a second offscreen canvas regenerated every 30 sim-steps or on layer-key change, then live glyphs (settlements, capitals stars, shock rings, sea lanes, ships, animated money coins, wind particles) drawn directly under a ctx pan/zoom transform. Hit-testing inverses that transform (screenToCanvas); drag-vs-click is disambiguated by a 3px threshold on panDragRef, wheel zoom is a native non-passive listener (React's synthetic onWheel being passive), double-click resets the view, and `jumpTo` (event-feed click-through) recenters the pan refs at ≥2.4× zoom. The globe path resamples the composited ImageData into a reused 25MB RGB buffer at ≤4Hz, bumping `globeVer` to force GlobeView updates since the buffer identity never changes.

React state drives the chrome: lens rail (lens + per-lens sub-mode memory in subMemRef → viewMode, also written straight into viewRef to avoid one frame of lag), right panel tabs (world/realms/peoples/faiths/tongues/inspect — panels read the mirror ref during render, re-rendered by setLiveStep at 30Hz and throttled psStats at ~5Hz), the settlement Inspect card (collapsible sections in psCardOpen; module-scope presentational components to keep stable identities), realm detail (realmSel + `selectRealm` worker message so the chronicle follows the inspected realm), and modal overlays (tech tree, chronicle with true-record/scribes toggle round-tripped through the worker, dynasty with an explicit frozen snapshot). Effects forward playing/speed, selection, viewMode and dynasty-open to the worker; both workers are terminated on unmount. Save = worker serializes and the main thread downloads via downloadSaveRef; Load = parse meta, restore preset/oceanLevel/seed and regenerate (worker rebuilds the sim from the save's own meta). Keyboard (Space/1-9/Escape) is a window listener deliberately re-subscribed every render for fresh closures. Cardinal-rules-wise this file is clean: the displayed year/era are derived read-only labels (displayYearStr over the emergent `_eraAt` timeline, leadingEra from worker stats), HISTORY_INTERVAL and the snapshot/overlay regen cadences are pure amortization, and no mechanic is driven from the UI clock. Overall the architecture is sound and deliberate; the defects found are wiring gaps (a dropped snapshot field, missed cache/listener resets, paused-mode render starvation) rather than structural problems.


## ui-shell
_Files: src/App.jsx, src/main.jsx, src/GlobeView.jsx, src/TuningPanel.jsx, src/SimLevers.jsx, src/paramDefs.js, src/mapImport.js, src/atlasUI.css, index.html, vite.config.js, eslint.config.js_

The sim-lever plumbing is schema-driven and sound by construction: src/sim/peopleSim/tuning.js declares TUNING_SCHEMA (~140 levers with key/label/desc/min/max/step/def), builds DEFAULTS from it, and exports a live mutable `T` that every sim module reads at use time, plus applyTuning (whitelisted keys, finite numbers only) and a TUNING_VERSION stamp for cache invalidation. Overall verdict for this slice: the architecture is genuinely good — schema-driven levers, worker isolation, reuse-conscious hot paths — and none of the findings violate the cardinal rules (the UI layer contains no time gates and no outcome-fitting; the closest thing is the globe's saturation hack papering over the missing sRGB tag).

## Cross-cutting hunt verdicts


### hunt-time-gates

Search method: I grepped every read of `.step` across src/ (211 hits in src/sim alone, all reviewed), plus `stepToYear`/`yearToStep`, `year`, `era`, `age`, `elapsed`, `Date.now`/`performance.now`, and every `step >/</>=` comparison, then read the surrounding mechanism for each hit to classify it as (a) per-tick cadence (`step % INTERVAL`), (b) display/bookkeeping (event timestamps, foundedStep records, chronicle text), (c) event-anchored duration (`world.step - stampedAt < WINDOW`), or (d) a genuine content gate on elapsed time. Files with step reads: index.js, settlement.js, conquest.js, armies.js, crystallize.js, shocks.js, faiths.js, cultures.js, dynasties.js, sea.js, roads.js, inflation.js, countryTerritory.js, transport.js, chronicle.js, historiography.js, events.js, entities.js, invariants.js, language.js, persist.js, plus UI files (display-only).

Overall verdict: the codebase takes the cardinal rule seriously and has visibly refactored away its historical violations. The big time-keyed systems are now genuinely emergent: the "era" is derived from tech ownership (tech.js techState reads knowledge thresholds; ERAS labels are display-only, including the era.reached chronicle event); the display calendar (calendar.js) is anchored to `world._eraAt` — the step each emergent era was reached — and is read by nothing mechanical; the dynasty clock (dynYear) is a deliberately uniform second clock used only for lifespans/reign durations, which are event-anchored. The former wall-clock gates (demographic population anchor, identity salience in cohesion.js, hinterland claim reach) all now read `world._civYear`, a pseudo-year mapped from the LEADING capital's organization level (index.js:188–194, civYearFromOrg) — development drives the clock, not vice versa, and the code comments document the old two-clock bugs at each site. Cooldowns/graces (conquest grace, truces, plague immunity, schism age, culture drift) are all anchored to a stamped event or state onset, which is legitimate mechanism. Per-tick cadences are cleanly separated via `step % _ivl(N)`.

Two genuine rule-1 violations survive: the settlement-coverage tempo in crystallize.js (a raw elapsed-time ramp, COVERAGE_RAMP=17000 steps "~renaissance", multiplying both crystallization and colonisation odds — directly contradicting countryTerritory.js's own written doctrine that coverage is never keyed on time) and the inflation baseline lock at `world.step >= 5000`. A third cross-cutting issue is not a rule violation but breaks the sim's own granularity contract: roughly a dozen duration windows (conquest grace family, colony supply, siege windows, homeland memory, tile-capture grace, climate cadence) are raw tick counts not divided by `world._dt`, while their siblings are — so SIM_GRANULARITY > 1 silently reshapes history.


### hunt-fitted-outcomes

Search method: I read CLAUDE.md and README in full, then swept all of src/ (sim core, peopleSim/, workers, WorldSim.jsx) and docs/ with layered greps for the rule-2 tells — named real-world regions (nile|egypt|china|indus|mesopotam|yangtze|sahara|levant|...), confession vocabulary (tuned|calibrated|target|to match|historical|hand-tuned), case-detection shapes (mask/flag conditionals granting bonuses), hard-coded map coordinates (fx/fy fractions), floors/clamps, and oversampling — then read every flagged site in context (state.js cradle seeding in full, crystallize.js in full, countryTerritory.js reach model, conquest.js capacity model, habitability.js, agriculture.js, cohesion.js, index.js anchor, territory/transport/sea/riverGen/moistureSolver/worldgen hot sections, tuning.js lever descriptions, all six docs). I also inspected the two commits named in the brief (425441e, 28315ce) via git show.

Overall verdict: the codebase is unusually self-aware about rule 2 and mostly clean. The hundreds of real-world names in comments are almost all MOTIVATION or VALIDATION references, not case-detection: habitability.js turns 'why sub-Saharan Africa stayed sparse' into pure climate-state signals (malaria/tsetse/arid functions of temp+moist, calibrated to the biome classifier's own scales); agriculture.js turns Diamond's thesis into landmass-connectivity and wet-tropic ceilings that work on any map; settlement.js's irrigation/alluvium multipliers are physical (arid x river x farm-tech); riverGen/moistureSolver/worldgen calibrate physics against real Earth (Gulf-Stream advection distance, Mediterranean winter storm track, Himalaya snowmelt) — legitimate for an Earth-physics model and expressed as mechanisms, not per-region patches. countryTerritory.js is a model citizen: its comments repeatedly refuse synthetic gates ('There is NO synthetic frontier close'), and its constants carry independent meanings (transport-cost budgets, resolution normalisation). The lever registry (tuning.js) documents mechanism rationale for nearly every constant, and tools/stylized.mjs institutionalises distribution-shaped (not named-outcome) validation.

The violations that remain cluster around ONE feature: making the Earth preset re-enact Earth. The hard-coded Nile/Mesopotamia/Yellow-River hearth table with per-site radii tightened specifically to overrule the emergent site score (default ON) is the clearest rule-2 breach; the floodplain spawn oversampling (un-normalized proposal bias plus a spacing constant tuned to a ~45% settlement-share reading) is its supporting cast. Independently, I found one genuine rule-1 breach that other sweeps may miss because it is inside the crystallisation code: COVERAGE_FLOOR/COVERAGE_RAMP ramps world settlement/colonisation rates on elapsed sim time (full at ~step 17000, '~renaissance'), directly contradicting the no-time-gate doctrine stated 30 lines away in countryTerritory.js. Two further borderline items are opt-in or indexed on development: the legacy historical-population pin (ANCHOR_POP, default off) and the CAP_GEO floodplain-capacity multiplier (default 0), plus the CIV_ORG_YEAR org→year table composed with year-anchored identity-salience ramps — rule-1 clean but a fitted stand-in for causes the sim already tracks.


### hunt-determinism

METHOD: I read rng.js, index.js (the tick loop), persist.js, peopleSimWorker.js, state.js, entities.js, events.js, cohesion.js, roads.js, armies.js and conquest.js in full or in large part, then swept ALL of src/ with targeted greps: Math.random / Date.now / performance.now / new Date; for-in and Object.keys/values/entries; every .sort() call site; localeCompare (none exist); every rng()/passRng/entityRng/mkRng/hash32 call site; and every iteration over world-level Maps/Sets (countries, polities, cultures, faiths, persons, dynasties, _warExhaust, _roadTiles, _flowTiles, ...). I then ran the full smoke suite (passes, 15.5s) — which incidentally exposed the co-located-hearths bug in its own log output.

RNG ARCHITECTURE (verified sound): there is deliberately NO shared sequential stream (state.js:70). All randomness derives from `hash32(world.seed, systemName, step-or-id)` through sfc32 — `passRng` gives a fresh per-(seed,system,step) stream, `entityRng` a stable per-(seed,system,id) stream, and one-shot decisions hash directly (e.g. `hash32(seed,\"slaveRevolt\",s.id,step)`). Streams are stateless across ticks, so there is legitimately no RNG state to serialize. I checked every call site: passes that share one stream consume draws in deterministic order — dynasties sorts country ids first (dynasties.js:822), faith conversion sorts settlements by id (faiths.js:264), crystallize/shocks/settlers iterate the settlements array. The only convention breach found is settlement.js:479, which omits the seed (finding). Worldgen (tectonicGen/worldgen/pipeline) uses single sequential streams from the world seed — deterministic per seed; language generation is fully hash-derived from (lang.seed, purpose, generation, n).

ORDERING: JS spec guarantees make the pervasive Object/Map/Set iteration safe here: object keys iterate in insertion order, Maps/Sets in insertion order, and every state-feeding container is built either from the `world.settlements` array (whose order round-trips through saves — settlements are saved and restored in array order) or from saved `[...map.entries()]` (order-preserving both ways). `rebuildCountries` derives the countries Map from settlements-array order; capitals break power ties by first-seen; the admin-load sort (`loads.sort((a,b)=>a.load-b.load)`), front priorities, and all other float-key sorts ride on spec-stable Array.sort with deterministic input order (and NaN comparator results coerce to +0 per spec, so even pathological ties can't reorder). Wall-clock appears only in pacing/profiling: the worker's STEP_BUDGET_MS break varies how many `stepPeopleSim(world,1)` calls run per slice, never what a step computes; Math.random exists only in UI (seed rolling, wind particles); TuningPanel's Date.now seeds a dev-tool parameter explorer whose chosen params are recorded concretely. Render-only side channels were verified inert: the identity field (tileCulId etc.) has no sim consumer, and the view flags (_wantMoneyFlows, _identityLens) gate only overlay construction.

VERDICT: run-determinism (same seed → same history, cross-machine) is in genuinely excellent shape — the discipline documented in rng.js is actually observed, and I found no true nondeterminism in any sim path. The weaknesses are all on the SAVE/LOAD axis: persist.js openly documents that some transients re-warm, but two pieces of genuine cross-tick HISTORY state fall outside both the save and the documented warm-cache list — active peace treaties (world._truces) and the per-country territorial claim ramp (world._cBudgetRamp) — plus smaller throttle state (road-plan backoffs, _warSeenAt). These make a loaded world diverge from its pre-save trajectory far more than 'mid-breath', and the smoke test can't see it because its determinism check compares only coarse aggregate stats and its save check only hashes the fields that were saved.


### hunt-save-load

Save/load is a "seed + dynamic state" design: saveWorld (src/sim/persist.js) never stores terrain — it records the generation identity (W/H/seed/preset/oceanLevel/tecParams, supplied by the caller's meta) plus the mutable civilization state, and loadWorld deterministically rebuilds the planet through the same pipeline.js the app uses, re-runs initPeopleSim (which re-seeds cradles), then wipes those fresh entities and lays the saved state over the rebuilt terrain. What is saved: step, the era timeline (_eraAt), demographic-anchor/climate scalars (_eraProd, _climIndex, _climShock, _popTotal), all seven id counters, tuning-lever DIFFS from defaults (reapplied after resetTuning, unknown keys safely ignored), settlements (an explicit SETT_FIELDS whitelist plus pos and knowledge), the five registries wholesale (polities, cultures, languages, faiths, persons, dynasties — all JSON-plain objects, verified), the append-only event log (reindexed on load), ships, four per-tile Int32/Float32 maps (roadQuality, roadFlow, _countryClaim, _countryOwner, _territoryOwner) as base64 typed arrays, finite mine reserves, five world tables (_warExhaust, _linkMoney, _inflRaw, _manpower, _plagueEvAt, _plagued), and per-settlement _seaReach. Randomness needs no serialization at all — rng.js derives every stream statelessly from (seed, system, step|id). Everything else is declared a "re-warm transient": loadWorld calls computeTerritory once and lets war fronts, sea lanes, road planning, spatial grids, countries, etc. rebuild on their own cadences. The smoke test verifies save→load→save hash identity (hashWorld samples settlement scalars, polity treasuries, event count, road samples) plus 500 clean resume ticks.

My method: I built the full inventory of mutable state by grepping every `world.X =` assignment across peopleSim (≈170 distinct fields) and every settlement/polity/country/culture/faith/person field assignment, then classified each as (a) saved, (b) deterministically rebuilt from terrain/seed/saved state (e.g. _tileValue, _agriCeil, _landComp, _claimNoise, _floodTiles, _mineTiles, _ancCulture via the ._anc tags, _evIndex via reindexEvents, _roadTiles rebuilt from roadQuality — this one even has an explicit "after a state load" comment), (c) an acceptable documented re-warm transient (fronts, alliances, _byId, _settGrid, sea scratch, road reach caches, per-tick money smoothers, identity field — explicitly render-only and derivable), or (d) silently lost cross-tick state that alters post-load behavior. The architecture is genuinely good — the transient/persistent split is mostly deliberate and commented, registries are JSON-safe by design, and the lazy `_tileValue` even cites load-coverage as a design goal.

But category (d) is real and has accumulated: the conquest tile-clock (_tileCapturedAt) is not saved at all — persist.js saves an orphaned field (`_capturedAt`) that nothing writes, so conquered hinterland rolls back to its pre-war owners at the moment of load; the cumulative soil-exhaustion ledger (_soilFatigue, on by default), the heritable organisation-aptitude ratchet (s._orgApt, on by default), signed peace treaties (_truces), the war-memory dedup map (_warSeenAt), the sim-facing inflation state (_inflP/_inflRef — the display map was saved instead), the country reach ramp (_cBudgetRamp), and the faith pacing cooldowns are all silently zeroed. Separately, the biggest post-load shock is a warm-up gap, not a lost field: world.countries stays empty for up to POLITY_INTERVAL(150)·G ticks, and in the default config every settlement's _eraProd devGate reads through it, so carrying capacity collapses world-wide for that window (with ANCHOR_POP=1 the anchor integrator actively erodes the restored _eraProd instead). The roundtrip hash test structurally cannot catch any of this because it only hashes what was saved. Tuning survives correctly; _checkInvariants/_dbgProfile/_identityLens are host flags — the tools re-set the first, but the worker forgets _identityLens on load. Versioning is a hard v==1 gate with no migration paths; intra-version drift relies on ??-defaults (safe forward, silently lossy backward).


### hunt-perf-memory

Method: read every file in src/sim/peopleSim (tick loop, settlement/territory/roads/trade/conquest/armies/sea/shocks/climate/food-hierarchy/identity/events/chronicle/historiography/persist), the worker (peopleSimWorker.js), the renderer (WorldSim.jsx draw pipeline, GlobeView), and persist/state; then profiled a real 12k-step run via tools/_harness.mjs with world._dbgProfile to get per-pass averages and maxima.

Architecture, performance-wise: the sim runs in a Web Worker that self-schedules steps under a 12 ms budget and posts a render snapshot ~30/s; big per-tile arrays go as transferables, split into a per-snapshot group (roadFlow, moneyFlows when the money view is open) and a "static" group re-sent every 6th snapshot (owner, roadQuality, countryClaim, identity fields, registries). The main thread keeps a read-only mirror and redraws per snapshot, but almost nothing is per-pixel per frame: the terrain base is cached per view-key in an offscreen canvas, the political/identity overlay is cached and regenerated only every 30 sim-steps, the globe texture rebuild is throttled to 4 Hz into a reused 25 MB buffer. Inside the tick, cost control is systematic and unusually disciplined: a per-tick id→settlement Map (clear+refill, no realloc); a spatial bucket grid (spatialGrid.js) that killed the old O(settlements²) near-queries; trade capped at MAX_PARTNERS=12 nearest peers so the trade/diffusion/urbanise loops are O(settlements×12); heavy recomputes amortised on intervals (territory 144, polity 150, conquest 50, sea 600, climate 20, soil 600, alliances 600) and per-settlement work staggered by id (KNOW_INTERVAL=8, DEV_STRIDE, TRADE_STRIDE with rates rescaled); road planning spread to exactly one settlement/tick with exponential backoff; per-settlement reach Dijkstras rotated over 120 ticks; pathfinding is A* with typed-array scratch + generation stamps (no O(N) clears); the polity pass Dijkstra uses a no-allocation heap (_PolHeap); road decay/paving sweeps iterate sparse Sets of live tiles, not the map; expensive derived values are memoised (exportValueOf per step, transport params per knowledge-object per tick via WeakMap).

Unbounded growth is well controlled: the event log is hard-capped (200k, compacts to 150k with reindex — prose is always rendered at read time from the structured log, chronicles store nothing); persons are pruned to ancestors-of-living; road tiles decay out of the road set; flow tiles drop below an epsilon; _linkMoney/_warExhaust/truces/inflation maps are cleaned of dead keys. What does grow forever — polities, cultures, faiths, languages, dynasties registries — is intentional persistent history and small per record. Saves serialize the dynamic state (settlement field whitelist, registries, full event log, six base64 tile maps ≈ several MB at Earth resolution) and rebuild terrain deterministically.

Overall verdict: this codebase has already been through several rounds of serious performance surgery (the comments document each), and the classic sins — per-tick O(n²), per-edge allocation, full-map sweeps every tick, unbounded logs — are essentially absent. Measured steady state at 240×120 tiles/43 settlements: ~2.5 ms/tick, dominated by the per-settlement update bucket, then trade. The remaining exposure is (a) a handful of fixed O(N)-in-map-size passes whose cost is 16× larger at the shipped 960×480 tile grid than at the test grid where they were tuned — the territory double-Dijkstra (measured 38 ms max at N=28.8k → plausibly 300-600 ms per pass at N=461k, a real snapshot stall), relaxClaim's rings×O(N) scans every 12 ticks, advanceFronts'/updateAlliances' full-grid scans at interval; (b) snapshot bandwidth (a 1.8 MB roadFlow copy 30×/s that is only consumed ~1×/s); (c) main-thread overlay rebuilds that paint ~10⁵ fillRects; and (d) one genuine save/load field mismatch (_capturedAt vs _tileCapturedAt). Findings below are ranked accordingly.
