# The 50,000-step run — and why almost every earlier finding was wrong

**Seed 8817, app grid (`W=960`, `tw=480`), 50,000 steps, all levers at defaults,
commit `21499f8`.** Displayed year 19400 AD — the calendar is cosmetic (FIRST CARDINAL
RULE) and is noted only because a run this long is the strongest available test that
nothing is secretly keyed to it. Nothing was: no mechanism misfired at any year.

This is the first run in the repo longer than ~21,000 steps. Every conclusion recorded
in `docs/observability.md` before it came from runs of **≤12,000 steps**, and the
overwhelming majority of them are artefacts of that horizon.

---

## 1. The headline: this world is not static, it was young

At 12,000 steps the sim claims **7%** of land. At 50,000 it claims **50.5%**. That
single fact explains nearly every reversal below: realms expanding into empty space do
not fragment, do not over-extend, do not fight over ground they can simply walk into,
and do not die.

| | 12,000 steps | 50,000 steps |
|---|---|---|
| land claimed | 7.4% | **50.5%** |
| realms alive | 33 | 62 |
| realms ever founded | 33 | **254** |
| settlements | 85 | 230 |
| population (field) | 8.8M | **32.4M** |

`realm.count` is **oscillating**, not rising: 4 → 70 with a 17.5× swing. It is a
turnover process, and at 12,000 steps we were watching only its opening.

## 2. Realms die. Constantly.

    polity.endedEver      521      deaths ever logged
    restoredEver          276      re-formed under the old id
    diedThenRestored      329      deaths invisible to endedNow
    lifespan p50        6,415 steps      max  46,213
    survival            82% @1k · 70% @4k · 53% @16k

**Half of all realms do not survive 16,000 steps.** The trace also reports 57 realms
that were born *and* died inside a single 2,500-step checkpoint interval — flagged
explicitly rather than silently dropped.

The earlier finding — *"no realm has ever ended, INVARIANT ZERO across four seeds"* —
was true of every run that produced it and false about the simulator.

`min 0.000` is a realm reduced to nothing. **51% of realms decline** against 16% at
12,000 steps.

## 4. Shape: ragged and fragmented, not discs

| | 12,000 steps | 50,000 steps |
|---|---|---|
| compactness p50 | 0.49 ("near-perfect discs") | **0.17** (largest realm **0.035**) |
| fragments p50 / max | 1.00 / 1.00 | **2 / 22** |
| enclaved holes max | ~0 | **19** |
| isolated realms | 43.8% | **3%** |

The claim that *"an exclave is structurally impossible"* — reasoned from the
connectivity release in `countryTerritory` — is simply false: the median realm holds
**two** disconnected pieces and one holds twenty-two. That was a structural inference
drawn from a distributional observation, the same error made about the vassal tree, and
it failed the same way.

## 5. The funnels reorder, and one reverses

**`secede` fires.** Previously *"0 passed ◄── NEVER FIRES ON ANY SEED"*:

    secede:  18,535 considered · 166 passed (0.9%)
        withinAdminBudget            10873   58.7%    (was 76.4%)
        garrisoned(recentConquest)    3957   21.3%
        infantColony                  2829   15.3%
        loyaltyStockNotYetSpent        439    2.4%
        peopleStillAttached            271    1.5%

The hysteresis brake said to have *"never once been the binding constraint"* binds 271
times. Realms do over-extend — after ~12,000 steps.

**`attack` reorders, but its sharpest finding survives.**

    coalitionDeterrence   72462   39.7%      (was 20.6%, now dominant)
    ✓ PASSED              54896   30.1%
    tradePeace            26541   14.5%
    warWeariness          17373    9.5%      (was 40.3%, now minor)
    outmatched               15    0.0%

War weariness fades and coalitions take over. But **`outmatched` holds at 0.0% — 15 of
182,630.** Military strength is essentially never what stops an attack, at any horizon
tested. That is the one funnel finding that survived contact with the long run, and it
is stronger for it.

**`capture` scales up**: 43.9% pass, **21,182 tiles taken** (was 23–28%).

**`roadPeer` reverses in the direction I explicitly declined to claim.** At 9,000 steps
`newRouteBudgetSpent` was 1.0%, so the report said carefully that the performance cap
throttled network *improvement*, not *formation*. At 50,000 it is **36.9%** — the
second-largest line. The caution was right for the horizon measured and wrong for the
system.

## 6. Networks: connected, layered, and mostly dependent

    TRIBUTARY TREE   28 bonds · depth max 2 · sub-vassals 3.6%
                     largest bloc 6 realms = 22.0% of all claimed land
                     DEPENDENT REALMS 45.2% OF THE WORLD
    TRADE NET        6,304 links over 228 towns · 1 component, 100% connected
                     busiest 10% of links carry 86.4% of flow
    REALM ADJACENCY  3 political worlds · largest 96.7%
    LIEGE TREE       166 bonds · depth max 3

Tribute pyramids form (depth 2, confirming the retraction made from `spread`), **45% of
realms are somebody's dependency**, and the trade network becomes a single fully
connected component with 86% of flow through 10% of links.

## 7. What genuinely is invariant

Not everything moved. These held at every horizon and on every seed tested:

* **`outmatched` ≈ 0** — attacks are deterred, never outgunned.
* **Cultures and languages never die** — `endedEver` = 0 for both, and there is no
  culture-death event in the codebase at all.
* **`_tradeReach.size` = 12** at p50 *and* max — a cap binding on every settlement in
  every world.

## 8. The methodological finding, which matters more than any of the above

Three gates were built this session and all three passed throughout:

* `coverage` — every value is measured. **True, and irrelevant here.**
* `spread` — the value is stable across seeds. **True, and misleading here**: it varies
  the seed and holds the horizon fixed, so "INVARIANT ZERO across four seeds" meant
  four seeds all run to 6,000 steps.
* `monotone` — no metric claims a history it cannot keep. **This one paid**: it caught
  `life.*.born` decreasing at step 17,500, the same defect as `died`, one field over,
  at a horizon nothing had previously reached.

**Error bars were built for the wrong axis.** Cross-seed variance was instrumented;
cross-horizon variance was not, and it dominated by an enormous margin. A finding in
this repo should be read as *"at the horizon it was measured at"* until shown otherwise.

The obvious next instrument is horizon sweeping — the same measurement at 6k / 12k /
25k / 50k, reporting which metrics are horizon-stable and which are not — bearing in
mind that this run alone cost roughly an hour of compute, so the gate has to be a
deliberate act rather than a pre-push reflex.
