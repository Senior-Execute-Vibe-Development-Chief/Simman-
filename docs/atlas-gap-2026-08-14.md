# The atlas gap (2026-08-14): why the map doesn't read as GeaCron

Owner report: *"the nations just dont LOOK realistic. it doesnt mirror history
(as seen at geacron.com) the way i want it to. why?"* — filed the day after the
variance arc closed with every distribution gate green. Both statements are
true, and the tension between them IS the diagnosis: **the distributions are
historical now; the map still isn't, because a historical map is not a
distribution — it is a hierarchy drawn in space.** At any GeaCron year one
empire owns the frame (Persia, Rome, the Abbasids each held 25–50% of the
state-held world), a narrow civilized belt concentrates everything else, the
sea carries whole empires, and the rest of the planet is tribal emptiness.

## The instrument

`tools/render_frames.mjs` (new): runs the Earth preset with the live dawn and
dumps the political map as a PNG every 3k steps plus a metrics line
(count/claimed%/top-1 share/top-5 areas/lnσ) — the sim's own GeaCron
time-slider, flippable frame by frame. `tools/render_blocview.mjs` (new):
re-renders one checkpoint under three paint conventions (sovereign / vassals
merged into suzerain / tribal fabric muted) to separate world-state gaps from
paint-semantics gaps. Both runs: seed 8817, `DAWN_LIVE=1`, tw=480 to 45k and
tw=960 twin.

## The measured defect

Top-1 realm's share of claimed land, tw=480/45k: **38% at the dawn (one cradle
realm on an empty map), then 13% → 8% → 4–6% for the entire rest of the run**
(2.6% trough at 36k). The world grows steadily MORE fragmented as it develops —
the exact inverse of history, which re-enters the 25–50% hegemon band again and
again from Akkad to Britain. At matched label years: year 0 largest realm 0.72M
km² (Rome: ~4M ringing the Med, Han ~6M); +750 largest 0.81M (Abbasids 11M).
Same shape at tw=960 (top share 4–7% throughout, docs/variance-arc). Meanwhile
polity count climbs 15 → 190 → 443 and the frames show the texture: 100–400
similar-size compact discs sprinkled uniformly over every habitable region —
sub-Saharan Africa carries a full simultaneous state-belt at −1500 (history:
Kush alone), statelets sit in the open Libyan desert, and from −1500 to +2250
the map's character never changes, only its density. The arc's far end
mismatches too: at 45k (industrial-grade development) only 36% of land is
claimed at tw=480 — the modern map's full partition (real ~90%, straight
treaty lines through the wastes) never arrives at fine grids; the recorded
waste treaty-partition candidate (shape-of-the-map §overnight) is this item.

**Controls.** Bloc-paint A/B at 24k/tw=480: merging vassals into suzerains
moved top-1 share 4.6% → 4.6% (7 bound polities); muting city-less tribal
polities left the frame visually unchanged. So at mid-game horizons the missing
hierarchy is genuinely absent from the world state, not hidden by paint (paint
matters late, when vassal stock and tribal fabric are large). What IS right and
should stay calibrated: dawn placement and order (Egypt+Kush on the Nile, a
Hittite-analog in Anatolia, Levant coast city-state specks, Europe empty at
−1500, Americas late, Australia never), the near-empty early map, the
claimed-fraction arc, the flows (lifespans/turnover/restorations), lnσ 0.7→1.8.

## Six causes, ranked by visual weight

1. **No empire ever owns an era.** The whole consolidation chain now exists
   (submit, cascade, momentum, SIEGE_STARVE, network inheritance) but arms only
   once dense adjacency + siege-grade war exist (~30k+), and the size law leans
   against giants: realms sit AT their demographic target, over-target land
   sheds most-remote-first, and reach ramps linearly with era for EVERYONE.
   History's giants were reach OUTLIERS — roads/sea/relay institutions that let
   one realm hold 3–5× its era's radius (and later overextend). No mechanism
   lets any realm's reach escape its era; consolidation's product is 4–5-member
   vassal blocs that self-arrest at the 5× bar (blocs face blocs — the
   variance-arc funnel, one level up, forever). docs/50k-run §7b recorded the
   reach hypothesis ("linear where history stepped"; no supply limit) — still
   unbuilt.
2. **Statehood diffuses like heat.** Cradle ORDER is right; the LAGS are
   compressed (Sahel arms ~1.1ky after the Nile vs ~2.5ky+ real; everything
   farmed matures within ~2ky of the first state vs 2–4.5ky staggered).
   Unpriced: temperate-forest soils before the plough (the recorded
   Europe-first gap), aridity that kept the deep desert/steppe stateless, the
   farming→state gap varying with caging. So no frame shows the signature: a
   small lit core against vast tribal dark.
3. **The sea is missing from history, at two levels.** Navigation is the
   stalled track (0.57 galleys under construction 0.99 — "flight before the
   compass", docs/50k-vs-real-history §4): no thalassocracy, no Med-ring (the
   amphibious lane waits), no exploration, no colonial partition. AND an
   overseas colony mints as a NEW sovereign polity (sea.js:877), so one color
   spanning an ocean — the most iconic colonial-atlas gestalt — is structurally
   impossible regardless of tech.
4. **Borders are cost-bisectors with noise, never the geography itself.**
   Rivers are the CHEAPEST tiles so realms straddle both banks — a Rhine/Danube
   frontier is impossible by construction; no watershed/crest device; no
   straight treaty lines through desert (the aspiration is a comment,
   countryTerritory.js ~:1243); exclaves severed every pass and corridors
   triple-suppressed (too clean for the medieval patchwork). The one genuinely
   historical piece: war fronts DO snap to ridges/rivers (armies.js terrainDef).
5. **The atlas layer fights the reading.** A vassal keeps its own color and
   assignCountryColors actively repels its hue from its suzerain's (the inverse
   of atlas convention); tribal land-nations paint as vibrantly as kingdoms; a
   realm's hue differs across lenses and drifts within a session. AND the new
   timeline scrubber is visually inert: the political overlay's rebuild gate
   (WorldSim.jsx ~:1697) never reads `_claimVer`/`_scrubClaim` (written :660,
   :2560, :4236; read only by labels.js:67), so dragging changes the year
   readout while the map keeps the cached present, repainting at most once on
   an unrelated invalidation. The scrubbed layer is also the raw
   `_countryOwner` — no atlas cull, re-solved colors, live labels over past
   borders.
6. **The year label lies about development** (known, cosmetic-by-doctrine, but
   it frames every GeaCron comparison): −5250 + 0.25·step drifts millennia from
   development, so "year −500" is compared against a world that is
   developmentally −2000. The era banner, not the year, is the honest anchor.

## What would move the look most (candidates, owner's call)

1. Decisive victory transfers TERRITORY, not just network — storm/cascade
   converting the fallen realm's provinces to the victor's (satrapization:
   vassal→province absorption at scale). Gives the classical act a protagonist.
2. Reach as an institution that compounds with conquest income — the
   Persia/Rome/Mongol outlier mechanism (and its overextension collapse).
3. Steeper secondary-state gradient — farming→state lag priced by
   caging/storability/soil-tech (plough gate for N-Europe; aridity veto for
   deep desert/steppe until pastoral tech).
4. The navigation accelerator + colonies keeping metropole identity (or bloc
   rendering) — unlocks the entire maritime half of the atlas.
5. Pure render: vassals in the suzerain's color family with a shared outer
   border, stateless fabric muted, stable per-realm hue, and the scrubber
   repaint fix (one gate read).

**Attribution — the finding worth keeping.** Reach is NOT the binding
constraint on empire size in this world; CAPACITY is. Extra radius grows a
realm's claim and its province count faster than its admin budget grows, so the
realm overextends and sheds, netting a flatter tail. That is consistent with
everything else already measured here: "46 of 58 realms sit exactly AT their
target" (shape-of-the-map), and mature size being CONTACT-set at 50k. So
docs/50k-run-2026-08-01.md §7b's long-standing recorded hypothesis —
"administrative reach ramps linearly where history stepped" — is now CLOSED by
measurement rather than left open: the premise (era-uniform reach) is true and
now demonstrably fixable, but fixing it alone does not build empires.

**The next lap this points to** is the other half of the historical mechanism,
and it is a different quantity: infrastructure that makes a FAR PROVINCE
CHEAPER TO HOLD faster than it makes new land claimable. The Roman road's real
work was not that Rome could reach Gaul — it was that Gaul stopped costing what
distance said it should. That is a change to the admin-load side (the strain
denominator), not to the radius, and `STATE_WORKS` is already the built, gated,
instrumented stock to drive it (`tools/probe_works.mjs`).

## Cause III attribution (the sea): why navigation stalls at 0.57 — two mechanisms, named at the line

The maritime CLAIM machinery is proven (probe_maritime: once nav matures, 19
of 38 realms cross water, archipelago empires span 25 landmasses, overseas
holdings recede when capacity falls). Everything hangs on `k.navigation`
reaching its gates (galleys 0.58 — the 50k world sat at 0.57! — compass
0.74, ocean 0.88). The growth law (settlement.js ~2144):

  nav += LEARN_BASE × 1.9 × sciMul × (1−nav) × (0.5+0.5·wa) × (1+constr·0.6…)

Two defects, one comparison: METALLURGY (rate 2.6) carries a DEMAND term
(needMetal) and fuel multipliers; MOBILITY (2.2) carries saddleLife ×3 for
peoples who LIVE the practice. NAVIGATION (1.9, the slowest) has NEITHER:
no demand term — no matter how profitable the sea lanes, practice never
accelerates (history's navigation exploded exactly when demand did: spice
routes, Atlantic fisheries, colonial rents) — and no live-the-sea
differential. Worse, the realm's naval capability reads the CAPITAL's
navigation (rebuildCountries: bNav = capital.knowledge.navigation feeds
RANGE_NAV and the FIELD_NAVAL embark), and capitals seat inland at the
biggest agrarian center — so a realm full of expert ports projects the
naval skill of its landlocked court. Rome's fleets were Greek; Spain's
pilots Genoese: the admiralty is the PORT'S institution.

**The wave (next session's spec, both grid-safe by construction):**
1. `SEA_DEMAND` — navigation's needSea term, symmetric with needMetal:
   practice scales with the settlement's sea-borne income share (the port
   trade/sea-lane value goods.js already books), so a world that profits by
   sea learns the sea.
2. `ADMIRALTY` — naval capability (RANGE_NAV, FIELD_NAVAL embark, amphib
   reach) reads the realm's LEADING PORT's navigation, not the capital's —
   entity knowledge, the grid-honest key class this session validated twice.
Win metrics, pre-registered: step at which galleys/compass/ocean gates fall
(vs 50k baseline "never"); probe_maritime cross-water realm count at 30k;
first Med-ring-shaped realm. Gates: full battery + resgate ×2 as always.
