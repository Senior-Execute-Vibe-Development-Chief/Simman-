From Standard to Flag

Walk a people's banner along the real arc of vexillological history — from the plainest cloth, up through the charged and quartered arms of the heraldic age, to a bold modern flag — always one length of cloth, only its design evolving. It grows more elaborate as its realm rises, then simplifies for the mass-made national flag, often carrying its colours forward the way many national flags took theirs from older arms. The banner through the ages

Each step is the same house, one age on. Click any to return to that age — growing onward from there.

The arc, and why it bends. A banner begins as bare cloth — a colour to be known by at a distance. As a realm grows literate and lordly, its banner takes on arms: a charge, a divided field, then the quarterings a house accretes through marriage and inheritance — heraldry at its most elaborate. Then the pressures reverse: a flag must read across open sea and be sewn by the thousand, and it comes to stand for a whole nation, not one bloodline — so the intricate achievement gives way to bold, simple, high-contrast geometry. Complexity rises, then falls. Here you drive that arc by hand; in the simulation it falls out of each realm's own development. Drift and Unite vary a house within an age; the ages themselves are the spine.
