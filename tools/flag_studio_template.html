The device through the ages

Each step is the same house, one age on — shield, then cloth, then flag. Click any to return to that age, growing onward from there.

The arc, and why it bends. A device begins as a coat of arms — a charge on a shield, to be known by in the press of battle. As a house grows lordly it takes on more: a divided field, then the quarterings a family accretes through marriage and inheritance — heraldry at its most elaborate. Then the device leaves the shield for cloth, flown as a banner over hall and host; and the pressures reverse — a flag must read across open sea and be sewn by the thousand, and it comes to stand for a whole nation, not one bloodline. So the intricate achievement gives way to bold, simple, high-contrast geometry. What survives the journey is what reads at a distance: above all the colours — a house's livery became its flag (the Dutch orange, the Belgian arms of Brabant) — and a bold ordinary like a cross; what falls away is the fine detail — the charges, the quarterings, the borders. Complexity rises, then falls; the form goes shield → banner → flag. Here you drive that arc by hand; in the simulation it falls out of each realm's own development. Drift and Unite vary a house within an age; the ages themselves are the spine.
